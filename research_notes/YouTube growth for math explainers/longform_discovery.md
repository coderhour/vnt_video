# YouTube long-form (16:9) discovery for small/new educational channels (2025–2026)

Notes on scope and sources. Primary sources: YouTube Help Center pages, read directly (CTR FAQ, A/B testing, monetization policy, altered/synthetic disclosure, upload schedule tips). Also the Feb 2026 TubeTalk interview with Rene Ritchie, read through its episode page summary. Todd Beaupré's 2025 interview is reported second-hand by Search Engine Journal. Most numbers on retention, length and traffic mix come from vendors or blogs (vidIQ, AIR Media-Tech, Retention Rabbit, assorted SEO blogs) and are flagged as non-official. I fetched no source on chapters, end screens, cards or playlists, so those are listed as gaps rather than filled from memory.

## 1. What signals YouTube says it uses (official) vs. creator folklore

### Takeaway
YouTube officially describes recommendations as "pulling" videos for each viewer based on predicted satisfaction. The inputs it names are watch behaviour plus likes, dislikes, survey responses, time of day and device. It denies that it suppresses small channels and says it is testing ways to get low-data videos exposure faster. CTR on its own is explicitly not the target: A/B tests pick winners by watch-time share, and YouTube treats high CTR with low view duration as a clickbait warning sign.

### Cited Findings
**Official / YouTube staff**
- Todd Beaupré (Senior Director, Growth & Discovery), interviewed by YouTube Liaison Rene Ritchie, as reported Jan 27 2025: the system "isn't so much about pushing it out as much as it's pulling." The home feed aims to show each viewer what suits them at that moment, and YouTube wants to know "how do they feel about the time they're spending" — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- Beaupré (same interview): satisfaction is measured with "likes, dislikes, these survey responses" and "a variety of different signals." The system "uses time of day and device." A video can become relevant again months later, possibly to a different audience. Dips are "natural," and metrics should be compared over "90 days or more." YouTube now applies LLM technology to understand what a video is about ("the expert chef" rather than "the memorized recipe"). CTR and watch time "remain important," with no specifics given — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- Beaupré on dubbing: creators who dub "at least 80%" of their watch time tend to do better, and translated titles and descriptions should be uploaded alongside the dubbed audio — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- Rene Ritchie, Feb 16 2026: "YouTube does not push videos for creators, YouTube pulls videos for viewers." Teams are testing ways to get low-data videos exposure faster, and homepage slots sometimes test videos with only a few dozen or a few hundred views. When creators say they are shadow-banned, most of their traffic is usually still coming from recommendations. Real reach limits come from copyright blocks, Community Guidelines violations and age-gating. On dislikes: "negative signals are negative." They mainly shape what that one viewer is recommended, but widespread dislikes do register — [TubeTalk ep. w/ Rene Ritchie](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18630508-shadow-banning-inauthentic-content-and-dislikes-with-youtube-s-rene-ritchie)
- Help Center CTR benchmark: "Half of all channels and videos on YouTube have an impressions CTR that can range between 2% and 10%." Home-page exposure naturally lowers CTR. Videos with few impressions, shown to narrower and more loyal audiences, often have higher CTR. "New videos or channels (like those less than a week old), or videos with fewer than 100 views can see an even wider range." End-screen and external impressions are not counted — [YouTube Help: Impressions & CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)
- Help Center clickbait heuristic, as quoted by secondary sources: high CTR combined with low average view duration and fewer impressions than expected suggests the thumbnail is clickbait — [OutlierKit summary](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-click-through-rate/), [Help FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)
- Metadata (2024 Liaison Q&A Shorts, flagged as older than 2025): title and description are the metadata that matter most. Tags have only a small effect; use a few, mainly for misspellings of the channel name or topic. Category is a minor factor and file names barely matter. Mixing Shorts, long-form and live on one channel does not confuse the algorithm — [Stan Ventures](https://www.stanventures.com/news/youtube-reveals-new-seo-priorities-756.md.txt), [Tubefilter Aug 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- Upload schedule: the official Help guidance stresses a pace you can keep up ("Is this frequency sustainable over the long term?") and consistent timing and content plans. It says nothing about frequency affecting the algorithm — [YouTube Help: Upload schedule tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979)

**Non-official / folklore and vendor data**
- vidIQ, study of ~10.2M channels: median monthly view and subscriber growth rose at every upload-frequency tier. vidIQ itself notes this is correlation, not proof that more uploads cause growth — [vidIQ upload frequency study](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/How-Often-Post-on-Youtube/)
- Tubefilter (2020, old): the algorithm rewards audience consistency more than upload cadence — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2020/10/08/creator-news-burnout-youtube-algorithm/)
- AIR Media-Tech case study: a partner channel halved its uploads and got 77% more views per video. This is a single anecdote from a vendor — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/half-the-uploads-77-more-views-per-video-entertainment-channel-case)
- vidIQ summaries of Beaupré: there is no built-in preference for creator size, and a cold-start video leans on metadata (title, description) until behavioural data arrives. These are second-hand — [vidIQ algorithm guide](https://vidiq.com/es/blog/post/understanding-youtube-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- The official model is: impressions are offered → the viewer clicks → the viewer watches → the viewer feels satisfied (surveys, likes and dislikes, coming back). Each step gates the next. CTR that isn't backed by watch time is penalised indirectly, through A/B test selection and the clickbait signal.
- "Small channels are suppressed" is officially denied. What actually happens is that a new channel's videos get small test batches, which grow only if the viewers in those batches respond well.
- Upload frequency is not an official ranking factor. Consistency is advice about building an audience habit, not an algorithm lever.

### Gaps
- I could not find an official 2025–2026 statement on ideal video length. A podcast summary attributes "as short as possible while still conveying your message" to a YouTube employee, but I could not verify it — [Recapio summary](https://recapio.com/digest/youtube-employee-reveals-how-to-grow-your-channel-by-think-media-podcast).
- I fetched no official source on chapters (auto vs. manual, and whether they help search or Key Moments), end screens, cards or playlists as discovery levers. Verify these separately before relying on them.
- I fetched no source on how captions or transcripts affect search ranking. Beaupré's LLM content-understanding remark suggests spoken content is understood directly, but that is an inference.

## 2. Title/thumbnail patterns for educational and puzzle content (incl. Test & Compare)

### Takeaway
YouTube's native Test & Compare tool can test up to three titles and/or thumbnails. It picks the winner by watch-time share, not CTR, so packaging that over-promises will lose. Veritasium's experiments, though dated (2019–2021), show that stronger, curiosity-driven packaging that still delivers on its promise ("legitbait") greatly increases reach for educational videos. I found no rigorous 2025–2026 data on faces vs. no faces, or on showing the problem in the thumbnail, for math or puzzle content.

### Cited Findings
- Test & Compare (official Help Center): you can test a title only, a thumbnail only, or a title + thumbnail pair, with "up to 3 different titles and thumbnails." The winner is the variant with the highest watch-time share, and results read Winner / Performed Same / Inconclusive; with no clear winner, the first variant is kept. Tests should finish within two weeks. Desktop Studio only, and Advanced Features are required. Not available for Shorts, made-for-kids, mature or private videos. Editing the title or thumbnail during a test stops it. YouTube advises testing older videos first, making variants clearly different (similar ones produce inconclusive results), and using thumbnails of at least 1280×720, since one below 720p downscales all variants to 480p. A small control group may see only the default — [YouTube Help: A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- Rollout: thumbnail testing was previewed at VidCon 2023 and widened through 2024. Title testing started as a phased rollout in 2025. Secondary sources say it reached all eligible creators in Dec 2025, but I have not confirmed this against a primary source — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-adds-title-testing-youtube-studio/753015/), [vidanalyze](https://vidanalyze.com/news/youtube-ab-testing-for-titles-thumbnails-2026-guide)
- YouTube's stated reason for judging on watch time is to steer creators away from clickbait that fails to hold attention — [thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/) (vendor, non-official)
- Veritasium, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective" (Aug 2021, older): Muller separates "legitbait" (enticing but delivered) from "clicktraps" (misleading). Re-packaging a video with a more enticing title and thumbnail produced dramatically more views. His stated strategy is to use high-level clickbait to carry educational content further. These details come mostly from secondary or AI-generated summaries — [Veritasium page](https://www.veritasium.com/videos/2021/8/17/we-need-to-talk-about-clickbait), [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)
- Veritasium, "My Video Went Viral. Here's Why" (2019, older): a viral video needs a high CTR — [rosetta.to transcript summary](https://rosetta.to/u/veritasium/my-video-went-viral-here-s-why)
- Vendor CTR tiers (non-official): under about 4% signals weak packaging, over about 7% strong. Another tool calls 2–5% "average" and 5–10% "good." These are not YouTube thresholds — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-click-through-rate/), [Satura CTR calculator](https://saturaai.com/youtube-ctr-calculator)

### Inferences
- For a puzzle or math video, showing the problem (a clean figure plus a short question) in the thumbnail and posing a curiosity gap in the title ("Most students get this wrong", "Why is the answer not 12?") fits the legitbait model. It works only if the video resolves the gap quickly, because the A/B winner and recommendations are both judged on watch time. This is an inference; I found no 2025–2026 study specific to puzzle content.
- Test & Compare is the right tool to settle faces vs. no faces and problem-shown vs. hook-text for this specific channel. Run the tests on back-catalogue videos, as YouTube recommends, and make the variants clearly different.
- With little traffic, tests on a new channel will often come back "Inconclusive." Test & Compare becomes more useful once videos get steady impressions.

### Gaps
- I found no credible 2025–2026 data on face vs. no-face thumbnails for educational content.
- I did not reach Paddy Galloway's packaging analyses for educational channels; the search returned nothing relevant.
- I found no case study of AMC or competition-math channels and their packaging.

## 3. How a ~0-subscriber channel gets initial traction (search-first vs browse; hook, retention, length)

### Takeaway
Officially, new videos get small test exposures (homepage tests with a few dozen to a few hundred views), and new channels should expect very noisy CTR. Non-official sources consistently say Search and External traffic dominate under about 1,000 subscribers, because Browse and Suggested need behavioural data first. They also say educational "how-to" channels draw an unusually large share of traffic from Search (30–50%). Retention benchmarks are vendor numbers only: about 50–70% of viewers still watching at the 30-second mark is "solid," and educational videos average about 42% retention against about 24% platform-wide.

### Cited Findings
- Official: new channels or videos under a week old, or with fewer than 100 views, see an even wider CTR range than the 2–10% norm, so don't react to early numbers — [YouTube Help CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)
- Official, Ritchie Feb 2026: YouTube is testing ways for videos with little data to get exposure faster. The home page sometimes tests videos with only a few dozen or a few hundred views — [TubeTalk](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18630508-shadow-banning-inauthentic-content-and-dislikes-with-youtube-s-rene-ritchie)
- Official, Beaupré 2025: older videos can find new audiences months later, so judge performance over 90 days or more — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- Non-official: for channels under about 1k subscribers, "Search and External often dominate, since Browse and Suggested need watch-history signal" — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-traffic-sources)
- Non-official: search is "the most controllable and predictable source." Videos that perform in search feed Suggested distribution, which then feeds Browse. One creator anecdote: after building about 50k subscribers from Search, they reintroduced Browse-style videos, and the subscriber base acted as the seed audience — [joyspace.ai](https://joyspace.ai/algorithm-war-browse-vs-search-traffic), [bosswallah](https://bosswallah.com/blog/creator-hub/youtube-search-vs-suggested-videos-where-should-creators-focus/)
- Non-official: educational/tutorial channels typically get about 30–50% of traffic from Search — [humbleandbrag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-traffic-sources)
- YouTube Studio's "Intro" retention metric is the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds. Vendor guidance calls 70%+ strong and 50–70% solid. These are rules of thumb, not studies — [subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/what-is-good-youtube-audience-retention), [tubelab](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention)
- Retention Rabbit 2025 report, cited second-hand only: platform average retention 23.7%, educational/how-to 42.1%. Secondary sites cite these figures inconsistently — [humbleandbrag retention benchmarks](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-benchmarks)
- Beaupré, cold start (second-hand via vidIQ): with no behavioural data, the system leans on metadata such as titles and descriptions — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/es/blog/post/understanding-youtube-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- For a new AMC-prep channel, search-first is the sensible start. Put the exact searchable phrase in the title and description (e.g. "2024 AMC 8 Problem 23", "AMC 10 incircle radius problem"), because people actually search for competition problems by year and number. Browse and Suggested reach should follow once viewers watch, and come back for, those search-found videos.
- Hook: state the problem and the stakes within the first 5–10 seconds and skip branded intros, since the 30-second Intro metric is what Studio puts in front of you. This is standard advice, not an official rule.
- Length: there is no official ideal. The video should be as long as the full solution needs, and retention is judged relative to length. Treat any "8–12 minute" folklore as unsupported; I found no 2025–2026 source for it.
- Playlists and series in challenge-number order give Suggested an obvious "next" video. This is an inference; no source was fetched on how playlists weigh in.

### Gaps
- I found no official breakdown of traffic-source mix for new channels.
- I found no credible 2025–2026 benchmark for average view duration or average percentage viewed in math explainers specifically.
- The effect of chapters, end screens and cards on session watch time is unsourced in these notes.

## 4. Policy on AI narration and templated "mass-produced" videos (reach and monetization risk)

### Takeaway
AI narration is not banned, does not need disclosure on its own, and is monetizable. YouTube says "it's not the tool that's important." The real risk is the YPP "inauthentic content" policy, renamed from "repetitious content" on July 15 2025. It targets videos that look made from a template and feel interchangeable, and it applies to the channel as a whole. A templated series is allowed only when each video has distinct substance and real educational value. Disclosure of altered/synthetic content is required only for realistic depictions. YouTube says disclosure itself does not reduce reach or monetization.

### Cited Findings
- July 15 2025 update: "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content," clarified to include "content that is repetitive or mass-produced." The page states: "Generic or repetitive content includes content that looks like it's made with a template." — [YouTube Help: YPP monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Not allowed: "highly repetitive" or interchangeable videos. A "highly similar storyline template" repeating the same situation and outcome. "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value." "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production." AI personas posing as human experts on health, legal, finance or political topics — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Allowed: the same intro and outro if "the bulk of your content is different." Recurring series where each video has "a distinct storyline, focus, or concept." AI help with scripts or backgrounds, as long as the result shows creative vision. The rule: "the substance of each video should be materially varied and deliver creative, educational, or other value." The policy page does not mention text-to-speech — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused-content policy (separate): content that "exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create" is not allowed. The policy applies at channel level and applies even with the original creator's permission — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- YouTube called the July 2025 change a "minor update." The content it covers was already ineligible for monetization, and channels that keep posting it risk removal from YPP — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/), [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- Ritchie, Feb 2026: "Inauthentic content doesn't apply specifically to AI." Monetization policies are the same whether a video uses a camera, stock footage, CGI or AI: "It's not the tool that's important." AI-generated audio, such as auto-dubbing, is monetizable. His self-test: does the video reflect your own voice and creativity, and would viewers recognise it as your work? — [TubeTalk](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18630508-shadow-banning-inauthentic-content-and-dislikes-with-youtube-s-rene-ritchie)
- Altered/synthetic disclosure (official): required when realistic content makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, alters footage of a real event or place, generates a realistic scene that never happened, or is AI music that is the video's main focus. Not required for unrealistic or animated content, minor edits (captions, filters, audio repair), cloning your own voice, or using AI for scripts, titles, thumbnails or infographics. "Disclosure alone won't limit a video's audience or affect its eligibility to earn money." Repeated failure to disclose can bring a manual label, content removal or YPP suspension. Set it in Studio under Attributes → "AI use." — [YouTube Help: Disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- The Help page does not directly cover synthetic narration in a non-cloned stock AI voice. A 2026 secondary guide reads the policy as saying a realistic AI voice alone does not need a label. NBC (2024) reported that plain TTS narration doesn't inherently require one unless it imitates a real person — [AIR Media-Tech 2026](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/youtube-says-will-require-creators-label-ai-content-rcna143937), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/youtube-lays-out-new-rules-for-realistic-ai-generated-videos-154248008.html)
- Labels may appear in the expanded description even for non-photorealistic AI content. Ritchie: minor cases get a note in the description, while sensitive topics get a label on the video itself — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [TubeTalk](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18630508-shadow-banning-inauthentic-content-and-dislikes-with-youtube-s-rene-ritchie)

### Inferences
- A channel of AI-voiced, Remotion-templated math explainers, each solving a different problem with original diagrams, traps and variants, matches the policy's allowed case: a recurring series with a distinct focus and clear educational value. The risk rises if videos start to feel interchangeable: the same structure and stock phrasing with only the numbers swapped, or a high volume of near-identical uploads. Varying hooks, visuals and pacing per problem, and keeping a consistent tutor persona and voice, both reduce that risk.
- Disclosure: animated math diagrams with a non-cloned AI voice that is not meant to pass as a real person most likely do not need the "altered or synthetic" label. Ticking "AI use: Yes" anyway costs nothing in reach, per YouTube's own statement. This is an inference from the Help page, which does not explicitly cover stock TTS.
- The inauthentic-content policy is a monetization (YPP) policy, not a ranking rule. I found no official statement that AI narration itself lowers recommendation reach. Any reach effect would come through viewer response: retention, dislikes and survey answers.

### Gaps
- I found no official statement or credible data on whether viewers' reaction to AI voices measurably affects retention or recommendations for educational content.
- I found no published examples of YPP rejections or removals of AI-narrated educational channels after July 2025.
