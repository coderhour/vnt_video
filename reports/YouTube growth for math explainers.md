# Fix the packaging before publishing the backlog

Vantee's views are limited by packaging and distribution, not by production or teaching quality. The repo has 83 verified English explainers, each with a short. Only 15 are public, and 68 rendered videos are waiting. Every published video goes out with a raw frame as its thumbnail, a title ending in an internal "Level N" label, a self-intro before the full question appears (median 17 s), no on-screen route from a short to its full lesson, and no analytics. YouTube's own guidance says a new video's first test audience is small and decides what comes next. It also says metadata carries a video until viewers react, watch time beats click-through, and the opening seconds of a long video have to deliver what the short promised. So the backlog should get designed thumbnails, search-first titles and a faster hook *before* it is published, not after. The calendar adds urgency. AMC 10B is on November 13, the AMC 8 sits on January 22, 2027, and 51 of the 68 unpublished explainers are AMC 8, so the next ten weeks are the backlog's natural launch window. Most of what circulates about Shorts length, posting ratios and conversion rates is third-party data, and a fair amount is folklore. Each proposal below is labelled with how strong its evidence is, and the first job is to start measuring so this channel's own numbers can replace the industry averages.

## YouTube pulls videos for viewers, so first impressions are what a new channel competes on

YouTube describes its recommendation system as pulling videos for each viewer rather than pushing videos for creators. It says this explicitly, and it denies that small channels are suppressed. YouTube Liaison Rene Ritchie said in February 2026 that **homepage slots sometimes test videos with only a few dozen or a few hundred views**, and that teams are working on getting low-data videos exposure faster ([TubeTalk](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18630508-shadow-banning-inauthentic-content-and-dislikes-with-youtube-s-rene-ritchie)). Todd Beaupré, YouTube's growth and discovery lead, names the satisfaction signals: likes, dislikes, survey responses, time of day and device. He tells creators to judge performance over **90 days or more**, because videos can find new audiences months later ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)). The result is a funnel: an impression, then a click, then watch time, then satisfaction. A new channel gets a small test batch at each step, and the batch grows only if those viewers respond well.

Two official details matter most for Vantee. First, click-through rate is not the goal on its own. YouTube's Test & Compare tool picks the winning title or thumbnail by **watch-time share, not CTR** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)). The CTR help page also says half of all channels sit between **2% and 10%** impressions CTR, and that videos under a week old or under 100 views swing even more widely ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)). The right target is honest curiosity that the video then pays off. Veritasium calls this "legitbait," as opposed to clickbait that traps the viewer. That evidence is dated (2021) and mostly second-hand, but it is the clearest case study of re-packaging an educational video ([Veritasium](https://www.veritasium.com/videos/2021/8/17/we-need-to-talk-about-clickbait)). Second, before viewer data exists, metadata does the work. YouTube staff rank **title and description as the metadata that matters most**, with tags a minor factor and category a minor one ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/), 2024). Beaupré, as reported second-hand, says the system leans on titles and descriptions when a video starts cold ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/es/blog/post/understanding-youtube-algorithm/)). Third-party sources agree that channels under about 1,000 subscribers get most of their traffic from Search and external links, because Browse and Suggested need watch history first ([ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-traffic-sources)). They also report that tutorial channels typically get **30–50% of traffic from Search** ([humbleandbrag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-traffic-sources)). Those are vendor figures, but they point the same way as the official cold-start comment.

Upload frequency is not an official ranking factor. YouTube's guidance asks only whether a pace is sustainable over the long term ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979)). vidIQ's 10-million-channel study links more uploads with more growth, but vidIQ itself calls this correlation, not cause ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/How-Often-Post-on-Youtube/)). vidIQ also reports that channels created in 2025 reached 1,000 subscribers after a **median of 46 uploads when at least 95% were long-form**, against 90 uploads for Shorts-heavy channels ([vidIQ via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)). By that measure Vantee's backlog alone is enough to reach the threshold, provided each upload is packaged to win its test batch.

## The current pipeline gets the teaching right and the shop window wrong

The audit of the repo shows careful work on correctness and accessibility. Every video has an independent solve, a brute-force `verify.py`, word-level TTS checks, an Opus review, 9–12 chapters, burned-in captions, -14 LUFS audio, and playlists. The weak spots are all in what a viewer sees before deciding to watch. The thumbnail is `poster.jpg`, a single `ffmpeg` frame grabbed at a fixed second of the question scene ([render.sh](/home/brad/code/vnt_video/.claude/skills/math-explainer/scripts/render.sh)). It includes paragraph text, choice chips and the burned-in caption bar. In `put()` in `tools/youtube.py`, a thumbnail upload failure only prints "thumbnail not set" to stderr and records nothing ([youtube.py](/home/brad/code/vnt_video/tools/youtube.py)). So nobody knows whether any custom thumbnail is actually live, and shorts are uploaded with none. Titles follow the template "Can You Find All 30 Squares? | AMC 8-Style Level 5" ([math-explainer SKILL.md](/home/brad/code/vnt_video/.claude/skills/math-explainer/SKILL.md) §7). That spends the end of the title on a difficulty label nobody searches for. Several titles also have no topic words at all ("Where Do the Lines Cross?", "The Number You Can Never Make"). Tags are the same six defaults on every video, and shorts reuse the full video's tags.

The opening has a similar problem. The skill asks for a hook "in under 10 s", but the full question appears at a **median of 17 s**, after a third line that is always "Hi, I'm Vantee!" ([SKILL.md](/home/brad/code/vnt_video/.claude/skills/math-explainer/SKILL.md) §3, step 1). After that come a "What are we asked?" restatement and a 10-second pause card. That makes roughly 30–40 seconds of low-motion time within the first ninety seconds. It is a judgment call, since no retention data exists yet, but it falls in the window that Studio's "Intro" metric (share of viewers still watching at 30 s) measures. Vendor rules of thumb call **70%+ at 30 s strong and 50–70% solid** ([subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/what-is-good-youtube-audience-retention)). The two skills also contradict each other. math-explainer suggests "most kids get 26 here…" as a hook, while math-short forbids inventing statistics. Shipped intros ("Most kids find twenty") follow the explainer skill. In a niche where commenters publicly correct Michael Penn's solution to 2025 AMC 10A Problem 25 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Svl1KYiW7Ts)), accuracy is what earns credibility, and an invented statistic undercuts it.

Calls to action are missing from both formats. No full-video narration says subscribe, comment or vantee.ai. The sign-off card is on screen for only about 6–7 seconds, and nothing in the repo sets end screens or cards. Shorts push viewers to vantee.ai, an off-platform link that viewers can't tap. The only clickable path from a short to its full lesson is YouTube's Related video link, which `math-short` says must be set by hand in Studio. meta.json records no field for it, so nobody knows whether any of the 14 published shorts has one.

## Shorts are a bridge to long-form, and YouTube now says exactly how to build it

YouTube's July 14, 2026 blog post on turning Shorts views into long-form growth is the strongest evidence in this research for the short skill. It lists four practices: **add a Related video matched to viewer intent, put a spoken and visual CTA in the final 5 s, make the long video deliver the short's promise in its first 5–10 s "with no slow logo or intro"**, and optionally try vertical live ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)). The Related video appears as "a clickable link below your channel handle" in the Shorts player ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)). Links in Shorts descriptions and comments have not been clickable since 2023 ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463)). Third parties put Related-video click-through **under 1%, sometimes around 0.05%** ([Spotter](https://spottercommunity.beehiiv.com/p/related-shorts)). That sounds small, but it is the only on-screen route to the full lesson. The third tip also applies to the explainers: Vantee's long-form intro is exactly the "slow intro" YouTube warns against for viewers arriving from a short.

The math-short design already matches third-party consensus on several points. It shows a trap answer from frame 0, prompts a comment ("Comment your letter"), captions every word, and loops cleanly back to the start. The viral "can you solve this" math format works on the same comment-debate mechanism ([example](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/S7hmXlRd2ZQ)). Two things are open to question. On length, quso.ai's 108,138-Short sample finds median views **peaking at 11–20 s (901) and falling to 347 at 31–45 s** ([quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/youtube-shorts-length)). Other studies disagree, favoring 13 s and 60 s, or 50–60 s ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/)). YouTube's Shorts lead says only "to what length do you need to tell your story?" ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), 2023). A median of 38.6 s is defensible for a problem that needs setting up, but it is worth testing shorter. On ratio, AIR Media-Tech's 18,000-channel study finds education channels that mix in Shorts out-grow long-only channels (**113K vs 82.2K median subscribers at 100K–1M**), with the sweet spot at **25–35% Shorts** and decline above 55% ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)). Vantee's one-to-one pairing is about 50%. That finding is correlational and not specific to math, so treat it as a caution, not a rule. A Short also needs about **505 views per subscriber against about 106 for long-form** ([vidIQ via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)), so Shorts should be judged by the long-form views and app sign-ups they drive, not by their own view counts.

Two more official points affect the publishing tool. Since March 31, 2025, a Short view counts on every start or replay. Since August 24, 2026, every format counts a view from the first frame, while CTR, view duration and retention are still calculated on engaged views ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)). So compare engaged views, not raw views. Separately, Shorts lead Todd Sherman warned in 2023 that deleting and reposting a Short risks being treated as spam ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). The "Republish short" button does something close to this, since it uploads a new short and sets the old one private, so it should stay rare.

## AMC calendars and search phrasing point to topic-first, AMC 8-heavy publishing

The niche has no dominant video brand. Art of Problem Solving, the category authority, has about **113K subscribers, grew 1.8% last year and posts about 0.2 videos a week** ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/artofproblemsolving/)). The rest is a long tail of student and tutoring channels, much of it discovered through "Video Solution by…" links on AoPS Wiki problem pages ([AoPS Wiki](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/2025_AMC_10A_Problems/Problem_25)). Those videos win exact-match searches like "2019 AMC 10 A #25" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9klaWnZojq0)). Vantee writes original AMC-style problems to stay clear of MAA copyright, so it gives up that traffic by design. Its titles should therefore target the topic and intent searches that original problems can win, for example "AMC 10 geometry: incircle radius trap" or "AMC 8 counting practice problem", rather than relying on curiosity alone. The large general channels show how far the single-problem format can travel outside the contest pool. Mind Your Decisions built about **3.1M subscribers** on one-puzzle videos ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCHnj59g7jezwTy5GeL8EA_g)). It is not known whether Vantee's animation is a real advantage over competitors, because no competitor videos could be opened during research. Check that before building positioning on it.

The calendar matters more for sequencing than anything else in this report. AMC 10A was on November 5, 2026, and **AMC 10B is Friday, November 13, 2026**, per the China organizer's calendar ([AMC Club](https://en.amcclub.org.cn/blogs/571.html)). The repo's own uncommitted retitles agree on Nov 13. The **2027 AMC 8 is January 22, 2027** ([AMC Club](https://en.amcclub.org.cn/blogs/571.html)), after a 2026 testing window of January 22–30 ([Areteem](https://programs.areteem.org/?p=2677)). Of the 68 unpublished explainers, 51 are AMC 8 and 17 are AMC 10. So the AMC 10 push ends this week, and November 14 through January 21 is the AMC 8 prep run that the backlog was effectively built for. Searches right after each sitting go for answer keys and cutoffs, which are mostly served by blogs, not video ([Think Academy](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/?p=16269)). A short, accurate "AMC 10 cutoffs explained" video, published once MAA releases official numbers, fills a parent-facing gap at little cost. AoPS itself notes that AMC 10 practice doesn't substitute for AMC 8 practice ([AoPS Wiki](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_8)), which supports keeping the two contests in separate playlists, as the tool already does.

Policy risk is real but manageable. YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule targets content that "looks like it's made with a template." It allows recurring series where each video has "a distinct storyline, focus, or concept" and real educational value ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). Ritchie: "It's not the tool that's important," and AI audio is monetizable ([TubeTalk](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18630508-shadow-banning-inauthentic-content-and-dislikes-with-youtube-s-rene-ritchie)). Each Vantee video solves a different verified problem with original diagrams, so it fits the allowed case. The risk is the fixed hook formula, the identical "Hi, I'm Vantee" intro and the identical sign-off across 83 videos. Varying them helps retention and policy standing at the same time. The altered/synthetic-content label is required only for realistic depictions. YouTube says it is not required for animated content, and that **"disclosure alone won't limit a video's audience"** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). The Help page doesn't explicitly cover a stock TTS voice. Secondary sources read it as not requiring a label ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/youtube-says-will-require-creators-label-ai-content-rcna143937)).

## Measure first: three Studio numbers and one script before any test

The repo stores no performance data at all, and CLAUDE.md says "No stats automation." Until it does, every proposal below rests on industry averages. Three facts from official sources should shape expectations. CTR is noisy for videos under a week old or under 100 views ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)). Results should be read over 90 days ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)). Raw view counts are inflated by first-frame counting since August 24, 2026 ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)). Test & Compare on a channel this new will often come back "Inconclusive," and YouTube recommends testing older videos with clearly different variants ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)).

Start by hand this week. For each published full video, record impressions, impressions CTR, the Intro (30 s) retention figure, average percentage viewed and the traffic-source split from YouTube Studio. For each short, record viewed-vs-swiped-away ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)), engaged views and Related-video clicks. In the Vantee web analytics, pull the `utm_campaign=<slug>` sessions and sign-ups split by `utm_medium=video|short`, because those are the business outcome. Then add a `stats` subcommand to `tools/youtube.py` that calls `videos.list?part=statistics` for every `youtube_id` and `short.youtube_id`. Have it append dated snapshots to a `videos/stats.json` (or a `stats` array in meta.json) and join them to the existing `published_at` and `scheduled_at` fields, which were stored "to find the best time to publish" and have never been used. Public counts should work under the current `youtube` scope. CTR, retention and traffic sources come from the separate YouTube Analytics API, which most likely needs an extra read-only OAuth scope. Check that before building. The site could then show a views and CTR column next to each video's status.

## Prioritized proposals for the skills and the publishing tool

Evidence labels are used consistently. **Official** means YouTube Help, the YouTube Blog or named YouTube staff, marked "(dated)" when from 2023–2024. **3P data** means a vendor study with a sample. **3P anecdote** means a single case. **Inference** means reasoning from the repo or the notes with no direct source. Effort assumes the 68 rendered-but-unpublished videos are in scope. Metadata fixes also apply to the 15 published ones, but intro changes need re-TTS and a re-render.

**Gate before anything else: the made-for-kids setting.** `put()` sends `selfDeclaredMadeForKids: false` unless an environment flag is set, while the skills describe the audience as 10–14 year olds. This is a compliance decision, not a growth lever. Make it deliberately, using YouTube's own made-for-kids guidance. The only sourced effect in these notes is that **Test & Compare is unavailable for made-for-kids videos** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)). Its effects on comments, end screens and notifications were not researched and must be checked. Every comment, CTA and A/B proposal below assumes the videos are not made for kids. Framing the channel for students and parents (the description footer already speaks to parents) is consistent with that, but confirm it rather than assume it.

Item 6 is a business decision as well as a growth one. math-short's stated job is to send viewers to vantee.ai, and one `short.mp4` currently serves both YouTube and TikTok. Pointing the YouTube cut's on-screen CTA at the full lesson trades some app traffic for long-form watch time, and keeping a vantee.ai pill on TikTok means rendering a second variant (a prop, like the existing `cn` cut). Make that call explicitly rather than by default.

Some ideas are left out on purpose. The "8–12 minute" long-form rule has no 2025–2026 source, and the explainers' 5–7 minutes is as long as each solution needs. Posting-time optimization has no support from YouTube for Shorts. Community outreach on AoPS forums, Reddit and Discord, and collaborations with student channels like OmegaLearn, are plausible, but their norms and reach weren't verified. Treat them as experiments once the packaging is fixed. Multi-language audio for the `-zh` cuts follows Beaupré's dubbing advice (creators who dub at least 80% of watch time do better) ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)), but YouTube is blocked in mainland China, so the existing Xiaohongshu and WeChat route is the better use of those cuts.

## Conclusion

Vantee's bottleneck has moved from making videos to presenting them. The remaining work sits almost entirely in the parts of the pipeline the skills treat as afterthoughts: the poster frame, the title template, the first fifteen seconds, the short's last line and one manual Studio step. YouTube's own model rewards exactly the things Vantee already does well, which are honest promises, full payoffs and distinct problems, but only once a viewer clicks. A 68-video backlog published with the current packaging spends each video's one cold-start test on a raw frame and a "Level 5" suffix. The order of work follows from that. Decide the made-for-kids question first, then the cheap metadata and tracking changes (items 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7) before the next publish.
