# Big Triangle, Minus a Small One | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Here's a rectangle with a shaded shape inside. Can you find its area?

Lots of kids answer forty-eight. But that's NOT quite right!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's find the little piece they forgot.

## The question

Here's the question.

Rectangle ABCD is twelve by eight, and M is the midpoint of side CD.

The diagonal from A to C crosses the line from B to M at a point called P.

What is the area of the shaded region, APMD?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Let's break it down. A is at the bottom left. Then come B, C and D, going around.

M is halfway along the top, so each half of the top side is six.

The shaded shape has four corners: A, P, M and D.

It isn't a rectangle or a triangle. So how can we find its area?

## Solution 1: big triangle minus small

Welcome back! Here's our plan: start with a bigger shape we already know.

Triangle ACD is exactly half of the rectangle.

Twelve times eight is ninety-six. Half of that is forty-eight.

Our shaded shape is that triangle, with one small corner cut off: triangle PCM.

Triangle PCM has a base of six. But how tall is it?

Look at the triangle below it, PAB. Its base is twelve.

The top and bottom sides are parallel, so the angles match. The triangles are similar!

Six is half of twelve. So the top triangle is half as wide, so half as tall.

Together the two heights make eight. So split eight into one part and two parts.

Each part is eight over three. So triangle PCM is eight over three tall.

Its area is base times height, over two.

Six times eight over three is sixteen. Half of sixteen is eight.

Forty-eight minus eight is forty. The answer is forty, choice A!

## Solution 2: split it in two

Here's a second way. Let's cut the shaded shape from A to M.

Now we have two triangles: ADM on the left, and APM on the right.

Triangle ADM has legs six and eight. Six times eight is forty-eight, and half of that is twenty-four.

Triangle APM is part of a bigger triangle, AMC.

That one has base six and height eight. So its area is twenty-four, too.

The similar triangles cut the diagonal two parts to one. So AP is two of the three equal parts.

Both triangles share the tip at M, so they have the same height.

Their bases are on the diagonal, so the purple area is two of the three parts, too.

Two over three times twenty-four is sixteen. And twenty-four plus sixteen is forty again!

So when would you pick each way? Subtracting is fast when a big triangle is easy to see.

Splitting is great when a point cuts a line into known parts.

## Common traps

Choice C, forty-eight, is the big trap. It's all of triangle ACD. The corner was never cut off.

Choice B, forty-four, takes away only four. That's half the small triangle. Check its area!

Choice E, fifty-six, is the part NOT shaded: ninety-six minus forty is fifty-six.

Choice D, fifty-two, is too big. Our shape fits inside triangle ACD, so it's under forty-eight.

Here's a great habit: after you subtract, check which pieces are left.

## New rectangle: the answer

Let's check. The top base is five, and the bottom base is fifteen.

So the bottom triangle is three times as wide, and three times as tall.

Split the height of eight into one part and three parts. Each part is two.

So triangle PCM has base five and height two. Its area is five.

Triangle ACD is half of fifteen times eight, which is sixty. Sixty minus five is fifty-five!

Check by splitting: ADM is forty, and AMC is twenty.

AP is three of the four equal parts of the diagonal.

So the purple triangle is three quarters of twenty: fifteen.

Forty plus fifteen is fifty-five. If you found fifty-five, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

When two lines cross, look for similar triangles to find the crossing point.

Then take a big triangle, and subtract the small piece you don't want.
