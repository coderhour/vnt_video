## The question

Here's the question.

A rectangular floor is covered with square tiles, each one foot on a side.

The forty tiles along the edge are blue.

The other eighty tiles are white.

How many feet longer is the floor than it is wide?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

Forty blue plus eighty white makes one hundred twenty tiles in all.

Each tile is one foot, so the floor's length times its width is one hundred twenty.

We want the difference: length minus width.

## Solution 1: make a list

Welcome back! Let's make a list of floors with one hundred twenty tiles, and count each one's edge.

First, how do we count edge tiles? Take the whole top row and the whole bottom row.

Then add the two side columns, but skip their corners. Five minus two leaves three each.

On this small example, that's seven plus seven plus three plus three, twenty edge tiles.

Long, thin floors are almost all edge.

Sixty by two has one hundred twenty edge tiles. Forty by three has eighty-two.

As the floor gets squarer, the edge shrinks: sixty-four, fifty-four, forty-eight.

Fifteen by eight gives forty-two. So close!

Twelve by ten gives exactly forty. And the inside is ten by eight, eighty white tiles. Perfect!

Twelve minus ten is two. The floor is two feet longer. That's Choice B!

## Solution 2: the edge gives a sum

Here's a faster way.

The edge is two lengths plus two widths, minus the four corners we'd count twice.

So two lengths plus two widths, minus four, is forty.

Add four: two lengths plus two widths is forty-four. So length plus width is twenty-two.

Now find two numbers that add to twenty-two and multiply to one hundred twenty.

Eleven times eleven is one hundred twenty-one, just one too many.

Spread them apart by one each: twelve and ten.

Twelve times ten is one hundred twenty. Again, a difference of two!

When would you pick this way? When you know a sum and a product. That pins down the two numbers.

## Common traps

Now let's test every choice. We know length plus width is twenty-two.

A difference of one or three can't work.

Twenty-two minus an odd number is odd, and half of an odd number isn't whole.

A difference of four gives nine and thirteen. That's one hundred seventeen tiles, three short.

A difference of six gives eight and fourteen. That's only one hundred twelve tiles.

Only a difference of two gives ten and twelve, exactly one hundred twenty tiles.

## New floor: the answer

Let's check. Thirty-six plus sixty is ninety-six tiles in all.

Add the four corners to thirty-six. Then cut forty in half. Length plus width is twenty.

Ten times ten is one hundred, too many. Twelve times eight is ninety-six. That's it!

The inside is ten by six, sixty white tiles. It checks out.

Twelve minus eight is four. The floor is four feet longer!

If you got four, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Add four to the edge tiles, then cut it in half. That's length plus width.

Count every tile in the floor to get length times width.

Then find the pair of numbers that fits both.
