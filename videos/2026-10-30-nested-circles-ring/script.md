# Circle, Square, Circle: Find the Ring | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## The question

Here's the question.

A circle fits inside a square with side length eight.

A smaller square has its corners on that circle. A smaller circle fits inside that square.

What is the area of the shaded ring between the two circles?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what are we really looking for?

The ring is the big circle, take away the small circle.

So we need the area of each circle. And for that, we need each radius.

## Solution 1: find each radius

Welcome back! Let's start with the big circle.

It fits snugly in the square, so its diameter equals the side. That's eight.

So its radius is four. Pi times four squared is sixteen pi.

Now the small square. Its corners touch the big circle.

So its diagonal goes right across the circle. The diagonal is a diameter, eight!

The diagonal and two sides of the small square make a right triangle.

Pythagoras says side squared plus side squared equals eight squared, which is sixty-four.

So side squared is thirty-two. The side is root thirty-two.

Thirty-two is sixteen times two, so that's four root two.

The small circle fits snugly in the small square. So its diameter is four root two.

Its radius is half of that, two root two.

Two root two, squared, is four times two. That's eight. So the small circle is eight pi.

The ring is sixteen pi minus eight pi.

That's eight pi! The answer is B.

## Solution 2: fold and turn

Here's a clever way, with no root two at all.

In the picture, the small square's corners sit at the middle of each side of the big square.

Now fold the four corner triangles inward. They cover the small square exactly!

Each corner triangle is half of four times four. That's eight. Four of them make thirty-two.

So the small square is half of the big square. Thirty-two out of sixty-four.

Now turn the small square forty-five degrees. It's a shrunk copy of the big picture!

Same shape, so every area shrinks the same way. The square's area halved, so the circle's does too.

Half of sixteen pi is eight pi. That's the small circle.

So the ring is the other half. Eight pi again! The ring and the small circle are equal.

When would you pick this way? When shapes nest inside each other, look for a shrunk copy.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice C, twelve pi, is what you'd get with a small radius of two. Sixteen pi minus four pi.

But the small square's side isn't four, it's four root two. The AREA halves, not the radius.

Choice D, sixteen pi, is just the big circle. It forgets to take away the small one.

Choice E, twenty-four pi, adds the two circles instead of subtracting.

Choice A, four pi, halves one time too many. That's the area of the NEXT circle in, not our ring.

So here's a habit: the ring is the big circle MINUS the small circle.

## One level deeper: the answer

Let's check. The big circle has radius six. Pi times six squared is thirty-six pi.

Circle, square, circle: each time, the circle's area halves. So the middle circle is eighteen pi.

One more level in, and the smallest circle is nine pi.

Thirty-six pi minus nine pi is twenty-seven pi!

Double-check: the area went down to one fourth, so the radius is half of six. That's three.

Pi times three squared is nine pi. It matches!

If you found twenty-seven pi, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Square in a circle: its diagonal is the diameter. Circle in a square: its diameter is the side.

And circle, square, circle: the inner circle has exactly half the AREA of the outer one.
