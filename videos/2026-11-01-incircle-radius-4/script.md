## The question

A triangle has whole-number sides. Its inscribed circle has radius four.

The circle touches A B and A C at the points six from A.

What's the largest possible perimeter?

Your choices: forty-two, sixty, eighty-one, one hundred fifty, and one hundred sixty-two.

So: radius four, six from A to each touch point, and we want the BIGGEST perimeter.

## Name the sides

Welcome back! Circle fact: two tangent pieces from one point are always equal.

So both pieces at A are six. At corner B, call the two equal pieces y. At C, call them z.

Each side is two pieces: A B is six plus y, A C is six plus z, and B C is y plus z.

A B is whole, so y is whole. A C is whole, so z is whole.

Half the perimeter is one piece from each corner: six plus y plus z. Call it s.

## Area two ways

Now find the area two ways. Way one: join the center to each corner.

Three small triangles! Each has a side as its base, and the radius, four, as its height.

Adding them gives one over two times four times the perimeter. That's four times s.

Way two: Heron's formula. The area is the square root of s times s minus each side.

The magic: s minus a side leaves one tangent piece. That's six, y and z!

So Heron gives the square root of six times s times y times z.

Set the two areas equal, square, and divide by s.

Sixteen times s equals six y z. Halve it: eight times s equals three y z.

Put in six plus y plus z for s: forty-eight plus eight y plus eight z equals three y z.

## Solution 1: make a table

Let's get z alone. Move eight z to the left, then pull out the z.

So z equals eight y plus forty-eight, over three y minus eight.

The top is positive, so the bottom must be too: y is at least three.

Make a table! At y equals three, z is seventy-two over one, so seventy-two.

At four, z is eighty over four, which is twenty.

Five and six give eighty-eight over seven and ninety-six over ten. Not whole!

At seven, z is one hundred four over thirteen, which is eight.

At eight, z is seven: a flip. Now z is below y, so only flips are left.

So s equals eighty-one, thirty and twenty-one. Doubled: one hundred sixty-two, sixty and forty-two.

The biggest perimeter is one hundred sixty-two. That's choice E!

## Solution 2: factor it

Here's a slicker way: make a product. Multiply both sides by three.

The right side becomes one hundred forty-four. Now add sixty-four to both sides.

The left side factors! Three y minus eight, times three z minus eight, equals two hundred eight.

Each factor plus eight must divide by three. Let's test the factor pairs of two hundred eight.

Two and eight fail. One, four and thirteen pass, and so do their partners.

Same three triangles! One times two hundred eight gives y equals three, z equals seventy-two.

When would you pick this way? When you want every answer without wondering where to stop.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Forty-two and sixty are real triangles with radius four, just not the biggest.

Eighty-one equals s, only HALF the perimeter. That's the tiny slip!

One hundred fifty is what you'd get if you left out the two sixes at corner A.

## Radius 3: the answer

Let's check. The area is three times s, and also the square root of s times four y z.

Square, divide by s: nine times s equals four y z. Put in four plus y plus z for s.

Get z alone: z equals nine y plus thirty-six, over four y minus nine.

Again y is at least three. Three gives sixty-three over three, so z is twenty-one!

Four and five don't work. Six gives ninety over fifteen, which is six. Then z drops below y.

So s is twenty-eight or sixteen, perimeter fifty-six or thirty-two. The largest is fifty-six!

It's the seven, twenty-four, twenty-five right triangle. Fantastic work!
