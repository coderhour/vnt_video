# AMC 10 Scoring and Strategy: Why 14 Right Can Beat 16

## Intro

On the AMC 10, sixteen right answers can score LESS than fourteen.

Sounds wrong? It's all in the scoring.

Hi, I'm Vantee. Let's crack the AMC 10 scoring, and plan your test day.

## What is the AMC 10?

The AMC 10 is for students in grade ten or below.

It's held every November, in two versions, the A and the B, on different days.

You may take one A test and one B test. That's two chances.

No calculators. The questions start gentle, and get harder as you go.

## Format and scoring

Twenty-five questions, five choices each, in seventy-five minutes.

That's three minutes per question, on average.

A right answer earns six points. The top score is one hundred fifty.

A wrong answer earns zero.

But a blank earns one point five. Leaving it empty pays!

Now the puzzle makes sense. Sixteen right is ninety-six points. Nine wrong add nothing.

Fourteen right is eighty-four. Eleven blanks earn sixteen point five.

That's one hundred point five. Fewer right answers, higher score!

## Why 100 matters

Why does one hundred matter?

Right now, a score of one hundred or more on the AMC 10 qualifies you for the AIME.

The AIME is the next round. Fifteen questions in three hours.

Every answer is a whole number, from zero to nine hundred ninety-nine.

Fourteen right with no mistakes gets you there. You don't need to finish the test!

## To guess or not to guess

So when should you guess? Let's do the math.

Leaving a question blank is a sure one point five.

A wild guess is right one time in five. Six points times one fifth is one point two, on average.

One point two is less than one point five. So a wild guess loses.

Cross out one choice. Now you're right one time in four.

Six over four is one point five. That's a tie.

Cross out two choices. Now it's one time in three. Six over three is two points. Guessing wins!

The rule: if you can rule out two choices, guess. If not, leave it blank.

## Test-day plan

Plan your time. Aim to finish the first ten questions in about twenty minutes.

Accuracy beats speed. A careless slip on an easy question throws away six points.

Check your easy answers before you chase the hardest ones.

Read the last few questions too. One of them might be in your strong topic.

Use the answer choices: estimate, check the size, or plug a choice back in.

## Try one

Let's try one, AMC 10-style. Add up four copies of two to the tenth.

Choice A is two to the twelfth. B is two to the fortieth. C is eight to the tenth.

D is four to the fortieth. E is two to the fourteenth.

Check the size first. Two to the tenth is about one thousand, so the sum is about four thousand.

Two to the fortieth and four to the fortieth are giant. Cross them out.

Eight to the tenth is two to the thirtieth. Also giant. Cross it out.

Two to the fourteenth is over sixteen thousand. Too big.

That leaves two to the twelfth. Let's check it.

Four is two squared. Two squared times two to the tenth is two to the twelfth!

## How to prepare

To prepare, work through real past AMC 10 problems, with a seventy-five-minute timer.

Track your score. Where do your points come from? Where do you lose them?

Review every miss, and learn the big topics: algebra, geometry, counting, and number theory.
