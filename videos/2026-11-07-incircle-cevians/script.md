# Where Do These Two Lines Cross? | AMC 10-Style Level 4

## Intro

Two lines cross inside a triangle, right inside its circle. Exactly where do they cross?

A very tempting answer here is two to one. It's a trap, and you'll see why.

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's balance this triangle like a seesaw.

## The question

Triangle A B C has sides A B thirteen, B C fourteen, and C A fifteen.

The circle inside it touches B C at D, and C A at E. Segments A D and B E meet at P.

What is the ratio A P to P D? Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

So what are we really asked? How P splits A D into two parts, A P and P D.

We know all three sides. First, we need to know exactly where D and E sit.

## Step 1: equal tangents

Welcome back!

From each corner, the two pieces out to the touch points have the same length.

Here's the reason. Fold along the line from the corner to the circle's center. One piece lands on the other.

Call the pieces x from A, y from B, and z from C.

So x plus y is thirteen, y plus z is fourteen, z plus x is fifteen.

Add all three sides: forty-two. Each letter shows up twice, so x plus y plus z is twenty-one.

Take away B C, which is y plus z. That leaves x, twenty-one minus fourteen, which is seven.

In the same way, y is six, and z is eight.

So B D is six and D C is eight. C E is eight and E A is seven.

## Solution 1: mass points

Now for the fun part, mass points. Put a weight on each corner.

A seesaw balances when weight times distance is the same on both sides.

For B C to balance at D, B's weight times six must equal C's weight times eight.

For C A to balance at E, C's weight times eight must equal A's weight times seven.

So all three products match. Six, seven and eight all go into one hundred sixty-eight.

That makes B twenty-eight, C twenty-one, and A twenty-four.

Now merge B and C into one weight at D. Twenty-eight plus twenty-one is forty-nine.

A and C merge at E, so it balances on B E too. Both lines meet at P: twenty-four at A, forty-nine at D.

Twenty-four times A P equals forty-nine times P D. So A P to P D is forty-nine to twenty-four.

The answer is forty-nine to twenty-four! Choice D.

## Solution 2: a parallel line

Here's a second way, with a parallel line.

Through D, draw a line parallel to B E. It hits side C A at a new point, G.

In triangle C B E, D G is parallel to B E. So G splits C E just like D splits C B: eight to six.

That makes G E eight times six over fourteen, which is twenty-four over seven.

Now look at triangle A D G. P E is parallel to D G, so A P to P D equals A E to E G.

That's seven divided by twenty-four over seven, which is forty-nine over twenty-four. Same answer!

Pick this one when you'd rather not use weights.

## Common traps

Now, the traps.

A, twenty-four to forty-nine, is P D to A P. It's our answer, flipped.

B, seven to four, is what you'd get if G cut C E in half, making G E four.

Choice C, two to one, is what you'd get using the tangent lengths as weights: fourteen at D, seven at A.

But B C wouldn't balance. Six times six is thirty-six, but eight times eight is sixty-four.

E, fifteen to seven, is what you'd get from C A to A E. That's a ratio on a side, not on A D.

So here's a great habit. Check that every seesaw balances before you merge weights.

## 3-4-5: the answer

Tangent pieces first. Half the perimeter is six. So x is six minus four, which is two.

In the same way, y is one, and z is three.

So B D is one, D C is three, C E is three, and E A is two.

The products must match: B times one, C times three, A times two. Make them all six.

So B is six, C is two, and A is three. D gets six plus two, which is eight.

So A P to P D is eight to three!

## Recap

The big idea: equal tangents tell you exactly where the circle touches each side.

Then weights that balance each side turn those pieces into the ratio along A D.
