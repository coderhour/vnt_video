# They Meet Twice! How Far Apart? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Two friends walk toward each other, pass, turn around, and meet again.

One number unlocks this whole puzzle. It's the number THREE!

Hi there, I'm Vantee! Let's find out where that three comes from.

## The question

Ann and Bo start at the same time from towns A and B.

They walk toward each other on the same road, each at a steady speed.

They first meet 12 km from A.

Each keeps walking to the other town, turns around at once, and walks back.

They meet for the second time 10 km from A. How far apart are A and B?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Let's break it down. Ann and Bo keep the same speeds the whole time.

The first meeting is 12 km from A.

Each walker reaches the far town and turns around. Only then do they meet again, 10 km from A.

We want the length of the road. Let's call it d.

## Solution 1: count road lengths

Welcome back! Let's count how far they walk together, in whole road lengths.

At the first meeting, Ann's part and Bo's part fill the road exactly once.

So together they've walked one road length, and Ann's share is 12 km.

They walk on to the second meeting. Now let's line up every piece of both walks.

Ann's first piece goes the whole way to B. That's one road length.

Bo walked the whole road to A. That's a second road length.

Then Bo walked back from A to the meeting spot, and Ann walked back from B to it.

Together, those two pieces fill the road one more time. That makes three road lengths!

Here's the key. Their speeds never change, so each road length together takes the same time.

And in each one, Ann walks 12 km, just like the first time.

Three road lengths means Ann walks 3 times 12, which is 36 km.

Now let's split her walk of 36 km into two parts.

First she walks the whole road to B. That's d.

Then she walks back from B to the spot 10 km from A. That's d minus 10.

So d plus d minus 10 equals 36.

2d equals 46, so d equals 23 km. That's Choice D!

Let's walk it. Ann goes 23 km to B, then 13 back, ending 10 km from A!

## Solution 2: follow Bo

Here's a second way. Let's follow Bo instead.

At the first meeting, Bo has walked the rest of the road. That's d minus 12.

By the second meeting he walks 3 times as far, for the same reason as Ann: 3 groups of d minus 12.

He walked the whole road to A, then 10 km back. That's d plus 10.

So 3d minus 36 equals d plus 10.

2d equals 46 again, so d is 23. It matches!

Which way to pick? Follow the walker whose first distance you know. Here, that's Ann.

## Common traps

Let's test every choice. Ann always walks 36 km. Where does she end up?

Choice A, 18, is half of 36. That puts her back at A, not 10 km away.

Choice B, 21, comes from no step at all. She would end up 6 km from A.

Choice C, 22, just adds 12 and 10. She would end up 8 km from A.

Choice E, 26, says Ann walked back only 10. But she walks back from B, not from A!

With 26, she'd end up 16 km from A. Only 23 lands on 10!

## Variant: the answer

Let's check. Three road lengths again, so Ann walks 3 times 7, which is 21.

She walks the whole road to B, then 5 km back. That's d plus 5.

So d plus 5 equals 21, and d is 16 km!

Check with Bo. He walks 9 km at first, so 27 in all.

That's 16 to A, then 11 back, which is 5 km from B. It works!

If you found 16 km, amazing work!

Here's the big idea. By the first meeting, together they've walked the road once.

By the second meeting, it's three times. So each walker goes three times as far!
