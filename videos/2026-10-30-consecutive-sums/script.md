# Which Numbers Are Sums of Consecutive Numbers? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Nine is four plus five. Ten is one plus two plus three plus four.

But a few numbers can never be built this way. Which ones?

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's hunt down those stubborn numbers together.

## The question

How many whole numbers, from one to fifty,

can be written as the sum of two or more consecutive positive whole numbers?

For example, nine is four plus five.

Here are the five choices. Take a good look at them.

## What are we asked?

Consecutive means next to each other, with no gaps, like four, five and six.

We need at least two numbers in the run. Five on its own doesn't count.

Every number in the run must be positive, so no zero and no negative numbers.

So which numbers from one to fifty can we build, and how many are there?

## Solution 1: fill in the board

Welcome back! Let's make a list, one run length at a time, on a board from one to fifty.

Start with runs of two numbers. One plus two equals three. Two plus three equals five.

Two neighbors are one odd and one even, so their sum is always odd.

That lights up every odd number from three to forty-nine. Twenty-four numbers already!

Runs of three make every multiple of three from six up: eight new even numbers.

Runs of four start at ten and go up by four each time. Eight more new cells!

Runs of five go from fifteen up to fifty, but only twenty and forty are new.

Runs of six always add up to an odd number, so nothing new lights up.

Next, runs of seven numbers. One through seven makes twenty-eight. That one is new!

Runs of eight: two through nine makes forty-four. Another new one!

Runs of nine only make forty-five, and runs of ten start at fifty-five. Too big, so we stop.

Which cells are still dark? One, two, four, eight, sixteen and thirty-two.

One and two are too small, since the smallest run is one plus two, which is three.

So six numbers fail. Fifty minus six is forty-four!

## Solution 2: the odd factor rule

But WHY do those six numbers fail? Let's build runs out of blocks.

Here's four plus five plus six, as three stacks of blocks.

Move one block from the tallest stack to the shortest. Now every stack is five!

So a run with an odd length makes length times middle. Three times five is fifteen.

Now an even run: two plus three plus four plus five.

Pair the outside numbers, then the inside numbers. Each pair adds up to seven.

Each pair has one odd number and one even number, so every pair sum is odd.

So this total is two pairs times seven, which makes fourteen.

Either way, the total has an odd factor bigger than one: the length, or the pair sum.

Now look at our six numbers. They're all powers of two.

Split them into factors, and you only ever find twos. There's no odd factor at all!

So no run of numbers can ever make a power of two.

Every other number does have an odd factor, and we can use it to build a run.

Eighteen is three times six. So take three numbers around six: five, six and seven.

Forty-four is four times eleven. But eleven numbers around four would go below one.

So use eleven as the pair sum instead: four pairs that each add up to eleven.

Two plus nine, three plus eight, four plus seven, five plus six. That's two through nine!

So the powers of two are the only numbers that fail, and the answer is forty-four again.

## Common traps

Twenty-four only counts runs of two, the odd numbers. It forgot even ones like six and ten.

Forty-three crosses out seven powers of two, but sixty-four is past fifty!

Forty-five forgets that one can't be built. One is just one, all by itself.

Forty-seven crosses out only one, two and four. But eight, sixteen and thirty-two fail too.

Great habit: check until the runs get too big, and check each power of two is really in range.

## One to one hundred: the answer

Let's check. The numbers that fail are the powers of two.

One, two, four, eight, sixteen, thirty-two, and now sixty-four joins them.

The next power, one hundred twenty-eight, is past one hundred, so we stop there.

That's seven numbers that fail. One hundred minus seven is ninety-three!

Quick check: one hundred is five times twenty, so eighteen through twenty-two works.

## Recap

A run of two or more consecutive numbers always has an odd factor bigger than one.

So only the powers of two can't be built, even in a huge range. Find them, and take them away.
