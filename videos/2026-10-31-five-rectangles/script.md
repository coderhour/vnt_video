# Five Rectangles, Perimeter 66: Find the Area | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Five rectangles, one perimeter, and not a single side length given.

Can we still find the area of one little rectangle? We sure can!

## The question

Here's the question.

Five identical rectangles fit together to make a large rectangle, like this.

The large rectangle has a perimeter of sixty-six.

What is the area of one small rectangle?

Here are your five choices.

## What are we asked?

Identical means every small rectangle is the same size.

So each one has a short side and a long side, and they're the same for all five.

We want the area of one of them: short side times long side.

## Solution 1: build with blocks

Welcome back! Let's start by matching edges. Look at the top and the bottom of the big rectangle.

Across the top are two long sides. Across the bottom are three short sides.

They're the same width! So two long sides equal three short sides.

Here's a neat trick. Measure everything in equal blocks.

Say the short side is two blocks and the long side is three.

Check: two long sides make six blocks, and three short sides also make six. It fits!

So the big rectangle is six blocks wide.

Its height is one short side plus one long side. That's two plus three, five blocks.

Walk all the way around: six, plus five, plus six, plus five. That's twenty-two blocks.

Twenty-two blocks make sixty-six, so one block is sixty-six divided by twenty-two, which is three.

Now the short side is two times three, six. The long side is three times three, nine.

The area is six times nine, fifty-four. That's Choice C!

Check: the big rectangle is eighteen by fifteen. Two times thirty-three is sixty-six. Perfect!

## Solution 2: walk the edge

Here's another way. Let's walk around the edge and count the pieces.

The top has two long sides. The bottom has three short sides. Each side has one short and one long.

That's four long sides and five short sides, all adding to sixty-six.

Four long sides are twice two long sides. That's twice three short sides, which is six short sides.

So eleven short sides make sixty-six. One short side is six, and the long side is nine.

When would you pick this way? When you'd rather swap long sides for short ones than draw blocks.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the traps. Choice A, thirty-six, is six times six. That squares the short side.

Choice E, eighty-one, is nine times nine. That squares the long side. Our rectangle isn't a square!

Choice B, forty-five, is what you'd get from sides five and nine.

But three fives make fifteen, and two nines make eighteen.

Those don't match, so that rectangle can't fit the picture.

Choice D, seventy-two, could come from sides eight and nine.

Three eights make twenty-four, not eighteen. No fit either.

Of the choices, only six and nine fit, and they give a perimeter of sixty-six.

So the area is fifty-four.

## Seven rectangles: the answer

Let's check. Three long sides on top match four short sides below.

So use blocks again: short side three blocks, long side four. Both rows are twelve blocks wide.

The height is three plus four, seven blocks.

Around the edge: twelve, seven, twelve, seven.

Thirty-eight blocks make seventy-six, so one block is two.

The short side is six, and the long side is eight.

The area is six times eight, forty-eight!

If you got forty-eight, fantastic work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Match the edges to see how the short and long sides compare.

Then measure everything in equal blocks.

The big perimeter tells you how big one block is, and that unlocks the area.
