# The Biggest Triangle in a Square | AMC 10-Style Level 5

## Intro

Here's a square. How big a triangle can you fit inside it?

Most kids set it flat on the bottom. But that's NOT the biggest one!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's find out how to fit a bigger one.

## The question

Here's the question.

We have a square with side length one.

What is the area of the largest equilateral triangle that fits inside it?

Equilateral means all three sides are equal.

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what are we really looking for?

Three equal sides, and every corner must stay inside the square.

The easy one sits flat, with side one. Its area is root three over four.

That's about forty-three percent of the square.

But look at all this empty space above it. Can we use it?

## Solution 1: turn it in a corner

Welcome back! Here's our plan: push the triangle into a corner, and turn it.

Keep this corner pinned. As it turns, let it grow as big as it can.

Keep your eye on the side length.

It grows, and grows, and then it starts to shrink again.

The biggest one is right in the middle, at fifteen degrees.

It's perfectly balanced across the diagonal. Its two far corners touch two sides.

Now let's find its exact size.

Call this little gap on the right side x.

It's balanced, so the gap along the top is x too.

This side is the long side of a right triangle, with legs one and x.

So by Pythagoras, its square is one plus x squared.

The third side cuts off the top right corner. Both legs there are one minus x.

So its square is two times one minus x, all squared.

All three sides are equal, so these two must match!

Let's tidy up. Expand the right side, and move everything over.

We get x squared, minus four x, plus one, equals zero.

Here's a neat trick. Add three to both sides, and the left is a perfect square!

So x minus two, all squared, equals three.

That means x is two plus root three, or two minus root three.

Two plus root three is bigger than the whole square. So x is two minus root three.

Now for the area. The side squared is one plus x squared.

Put in x, and it works out to eight minus four root three.

An equilateral triangle's area is root three over four, times the side squared.

Careful here: root three times root three is three.

So the area is two root three, minus three!

That's about forty-six percent of the square. Bigger than forty-three!

## Solution 2: cut off the corners

Here's a second way to finish, without the equilateral area formula.

Start with the whole square. Its area is one.

Now cut away the three right triangles around our triangle.

The two long ones have legs one and x. Together, their area is x.

The corner one has two legs of one minus x.

One minus x is root three minus one. Square it, take half, and you get two minus root three.

And x is also two minus root three! So we cut that away twice.

One, minus two times two minus root three. That's two root three, minus three again!

When would you pick this way? To double-check, or if you forget the area formula.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

A is the flat triangle with side one. It fits, but it's not the biggest.

B forgets to square the side in the area formula.

D is a triangle exactly one unit tall. But then its base is too wide to fit!

Choice E uses the diagonal as a side. That pokes right out of the square!

So here's a great habit. Always check that your shape really fits.

## Bigger square: the answer

Let's check. Call the gap x again.

One side squared is four plus x squared.

The other is two times two minus x, all squared.

Set them equal and tidy up. x minus four, all squared, equals twelve.

So x is four minus two root three.

Now cut the corners. The square's area is four.

The two long triangles add up to eight minus four root three. And the corner one is the same!

So the area is four, minus two times that. Eight root three, minus twelve!

Neat check: that's four times our first answer. Double the side, and the area grows four times!

If you found eight root three minus twelve, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

To fit the biggest shape, push it into a corner, and turn it until it's balanced.

Then let the equal sides give you one equation.
