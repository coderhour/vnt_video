# Which Dots Are Exactly 5 Apart? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Twenty-five dots. How many pairs are exactly five units apart?

There are two sneaky traps hiding in this one.

Hi there, I'm Vantee! Let's hunt them down together.

## The question

Here's the question.

The twenty-five dots shown are spaced one unit apart in rows and columns.

How many pairs of dots are exactly five units apart?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look at them.

## What are we asked?

So what are we counting?

A pair is just two dots. We want them exactly five units apart.

Five means the straight-line distance, as if you used a ruler.

Going from one dot to the other, or back again, is still the same pair.

## Solution 1: list the slants

Welcome back! First idea: go five units straight across.

But look. A row of five dots has only four gaps. It's just four units long!

So no two dots in the same row, or the same column, are five apart.

So the pair must be on a slant. That slant is the long side of a right triangle.

Pythagoras says: across squared, plus down squared, equals five squared.

Five squared is twenty-five. Each step is at most four, since the grid is four units wide.

Let's make a list. Across zero leaves twenty-five for down squared. Down five is too big.

Across one or two leaves twenty-four or twenty-one. Neither one is a square.

Across three leaves sixteen, which is four squared! Across four leaves nine, three squared.

So every pair is three and four. It's the famous three, four, five triangle!

Which way can the slant point? Start at the upper dot and go down.

Three across and four down, to the right or to the left.

Or four across and three down, right or left again. That's four slants.

Three across and four down needs a box three wide and four tall.

It fits in two spots side by side, but only one top to bottom. So two places.

The other three slants fit in two places each, just the same.

Four slants, two places each. Four times two is eight! The answer is D.

## Solution 2: dot by dot

Here's a second way. Visit each dot, and count its partners five units away.

Take the top-left corner. It has two partners: three across, four down, or four across, three down.

The dot next to a corner, along the edge, has just one partner.

Every other dot has none. Even the middle dot is too close to everything!

Four corners with two each makes eight. Eight edge dots with one each makes eight more.

Eight plus eight is sixteen. But each pair got counted twice, once from each end.

Sixteen over two is eight. It matches!

When would you pick this way? It's a great double-check, and it shows where sixteen comes from.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Two is what you'd get if you found only one slant: three across and four down, to the right.

Four is what you'd get if you only drew slants going down to the right.

Six is what you'd get by halving the twelve dots that have a partner. But corners have TWO.

Sixteen counts every pair twice, once from each end. A pair is two dots, counted once.

And watch the fence posts! Five dots in a row make only four gaps.

## Bigger grid: the answer

Let's check. Now a row has six dots, so five gaps. Five units fit straight across!

Each row has exactly one such pair: its two end dots. Six rows give six pairs.

The six columns add six more pairs. That makes twelve straight pairs.

Now the slants. Three across, four down fits three ways side to side, two ways top to bottom.

Three times two is six places. The other three slants fit in six places each too.

Four slants, six places each. That's twenty-four slanted pairs.

Twelve plus twenty-four is thirty-six pairs!

If you found thirty-six, amazing work! Did you remember the straight ones?

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

To find pairs a set distance apart, first list the steps across and down that make it.

Then count the places each one fits, and count each pair only once.
