# Pour It There, Pour It Back: How Much Juice? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Pour some juice into water, stir, then pour some back.

How much of the drink is juice now? Lots of kids get mixed up here!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's follow the juice, one pour at a time.

## The question

Here's the question.

Jug A holds ten liters of water.

Jug B holds ten liters of a drink that is sixty percent juice.

Five liters are poured from jug B into jug A and stirred.

Then five liters are poured from jug A back into jug B.

What percent of the drink in jug B is now juice?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

There are two pours. First from B into A, then from A back into B.

Stirred means the drink is mixed evenly. Every scoop is the same.

At the end, we want the share of jug B that is juice.

## Solution 1: count liters of juice

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: count liters of juice, not percents.

At the start, jug A has no juice. Jug B has sixty percent of ten liters. That's six liters of juice.

First pour. Five liters is half of jug B.

So half of its juice moves too. Half of six is three liters of juice.

Now jug A has fifteen liters, with three liters of juice.

Jug B has five liters, with three liters of juice.

Jug A is three out of fifteen juice. That's one fifth, or twenty percent.

Second pour. Five liters is one third of jug A.

One third of its three liters of juice is one liter. That liter goes back to jug B.

Jug B is back to ten liters, with three plus one, four liters of juice.

Four out of ten is forty percent. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: juice never disappears

Here's a shortcut. Juice never disappears. There are six liters of juice in all, the whole time.

Pouring out of jug A doesn't change its mix.

It stays twenty percent juice. Fifteen minus five leaves ten liters.

So jug A keeps twenty percent of ten, two liters of juice.

The rest is in jug B: six minus two is four liters. Four out of ten, forty percent!

When would you pick this way? When one jug is easier to figure out. Then subtract from the total.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choice A, twenty percent, is the right number for the wrong jug.

That's jug A, not jug B!

Choice B, thirty percent, counts only the three liters left in jug B.

It forgets the liter of juice that came back.

Choice D, forty-five, is what you'd get by averaging trap B's thirty with the starting sixty.

Averaging percents doesn't work here.

Choice E, sixty percent, is where jug B started. But the pours traded some of its juice for water.

Only forty percent tracks every liter of juice.

## New mix: the answer

Let's check. Jug B starts with seventy-five percent of eight, six liters of juice.

Four liters is half of jug B, so three liters of juice go into jug A.

Jug A now has twelve liters, with three liters of juice.

Four liters is one third of jug A, so one liter of juice comes back.

Jug B has eight liters again, with three plus one, four liters of juice.

Four out of eight is fifty percent!

If you got fifty percent, wonderful work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Count liters of juice, not percents.

When you pour out part of a jug, that same part of its juice goes too.
