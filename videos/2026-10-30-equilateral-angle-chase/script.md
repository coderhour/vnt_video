## The question

Here's the question. Triangle A B C is equilateral.

Point D is on side B C, and point E is on side A C.

The little tick marks say A D and A E are equal.

Angle B A D is twenty degrees.

What is x, the angle E D C?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Equilateral means every angle is sixty degrees.

A D equals A E, so triangle A D E has two equal sides.

We want the small angle at D, between D E and side D C.

## Solution 1: the outside angle

Welcome back! Let's start at the top corner, A.

The whole angle at A is sixty. Angle B A D takes twenty of it.

So angle D A E is sixty minus twenty. That's forty degrees.

Now look at triangle A D E. It's isosceles: two sides are equal.

Side D E is the base. The angles at D and E, the base angles, are equal.

Its three angles add up to one hundred eighty. Take away forty, and one hundred forty is left.

Split it in half. Each base angle is seventy degrees.

Here's the clever step. Look at triangle A B D on the left.

Angle A D C sits outside that triangle, on the straight line B C.

An outside angle equals the two far inside angles added together.

Why? Triangle A B D adds up to one hundred eighty. So does the straight line at D.

Both include angle A D B, so the leftover parts must match.

That's angle B, sixty, plus angle B A D, twenty. So angle A D C is eighty.

Angle A D C is made of two parts: angle A D E, and our x.

So x is eighty minus seventy.

That's ten degrees! The answer is A.

## Solution 2: the straight line at E

Here's a second way. Angle A E D is seventy, and A E C is a straight line.

So angle D E C is one hundred eighty minus seventy. That's one hundred ten.

Now look at the small triangle D E C. Angle C is sixty.

So x is one hundred eighty, minus one hundred ten, minus sixty. That's ten again!

When would you pick this way? It's a great double-check, using a straight line and the triangle sum.

## The hidden pattern

Did you notice? Angle B A D was twenty, and x was ten. Exactly half!

Is that luck? Let's call angle B A D the letter a, and redo our steps.

Angle D A E is sixty minus a. So each base angle works out to sixty, plus half of a.

Angle A D C is sixty plus a. Subtract, and the two copies of sixty cancel.

So in this picture, x is ALWAYS half of angle B A D!

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Twenty is angle B A D. That's the number we were given, not x.

Forty is angle D A E, at the top. It's not the angle at D.

Thirty is what you would get if you used angle A D B instead.

Angle A D B is one hundred. One hundred minus seventy is thirty. But x is inside angle A D C, on the C side.

Fifteen is what you would get if A D cut angle A exactly in half. Then B A D is thirty, and half of thirty is fifteen. But ours is twenty.

So here's a great habit. Mark every angle you find on the picture as you go.

## New angle: the answer

Let's check. Angle D A E is sixty minus thirty-six. That's twenty-four.

One hundred eighty minus twenty-four is one hundred fifty-six. Half is seventy-eight.

Angle A D C is sixty plus thirty-six. That's ninety-six.

So x is ninety-six minus seventy-eight. That's eighteen degrees!

And the pattern agrees. Half of thirty-six is eighteen.

If you found eighteen, amazing work!

Find the angle at D with the outside angle trick.
