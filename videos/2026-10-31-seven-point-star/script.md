## The question

Here's the question.

Seven points are evenly spaced on a circle.

A star is drawn by joining each point to the second point after it, going around the circle.

What is the sum, in degrees, of the seven angles at the tips of the star?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Each line skips one point. A joins to C, C joins to E, and so on.

The star is perfectly even, so all seven tips are the same size.

We want the total of all seven tip angles.

## Solution 1: slices from the center

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: cut the circle into slices from the center.

Seven equal slices fill the whole turn. Call each slice angle s.

Seven copies of s make three hundred sixty degrees.

Now zoom in on the tip at A. Its two lines go to C and to F.

Draw lines from the center to A and to C. The angle at the center covers two slices, so it's two s.

Both of those lines are radii, so they're equal. Triangle O A C is isosceles.

Its angles add to one hundred eighty. Subtract two times the letter s, then split the rest in half.

So each base angle is ninety minus s.

The other side works the same way. Triangle O A F has a two s angle at the center too.

So its angle at A is also ninety minus s.

So the tip at A is ninety minus s, twice. That's one hundred eighty minus two s.

Seven tips make seven times one hundred eighty, minus fourteen times s.

That's one thousand two hundred sixty, minus fourteen s.

Fourteen times s is two times seven times s.

That's two times three hundred sixty, which is seven hundred twenty.

One thousand two hundred sixty minus seven hundred twenty is five hundred forty.

The tips add up to five hundred forty degrees. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: walk the pencil

Here's a really clever way. Put a pencil at A, pointing at C, and walk it around the star.

At each tip, the pencil turns to head down the next line.

The turn and the tip angle sit on a straight line, so together they make one hundred eighty.

Each line jumps two slices. Seven lines make fourteen slices: two full circles.

Watch the pencil go all the way round. It circles the center twice before it gets home!

Two full circles means two times three hundred sixty degrees of turning. That's seven hundred twenty.

At each of the seven tips, the tip plus the turn is one hundred eighty.

Seven times one hundred eighty is one thousand two hundred sixty.

The turns use up seven hundred twenty. So the tips get the rest.

One thousand two hundred sixty minus seven hundred twenty is five hundred forty again!

When would you pick this way? For any star, just count how many times the pencil circles the center.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A, one hundred eighty, is the famous answer for a five-point star. Seven points is a different star!

Choice E, nine hundred, is the angle total for an ordinary seven-sided shape.

The pencil would circle only once.

Choice D, seven hundred twenty, is the total turning of the pencil, not the tips.

Choice B, three hundred sixty, is one full turn.

That's what the turns of an ordinary shape add to, not a star's tips.

So here's a great habit. Don't use a rule you remember for a different shape. Check it.

## Pointier star: the answer

Let's walk the pencil. Each line now jumps three slices, and seven lines make twenty-one slices.

Twenty-one slices is three full circles.

So the pencil turns three times three hundred sixty, which is one thousand eighty degrees.

Tips plus turns is still seven times one hundred eighty, one thousand two hundred sixty.

One thousand two hundred sixty minus one thousand eighty is one hundred eighty degrees!

Here's the sneaky part. Pointier tips, so a smaller total. If you got one hundred eighty, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

At every corner, the tip and the pencil's turn add to one hundred eighty.

Count how many times the pencil circles the center. Times three hundred sixty is the total turning, and the tips get the rest.
