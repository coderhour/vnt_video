# Grab 4 Socks: How Many Sets Have a Pair? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Grab four socks from a drawer of five pairs. How often do you get a matching pair?

Counting the matches head-on gets messy. Counting the NON-matches is much easier.

Hello, I'm Vantee, and today we're sorting socks!

## The question

Here's the question.

A drawer has five pairs of socks, and each pair is a different color.

Zoe pulls out four socks.

How many sets of four socks contain at least one matching pair?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Ten socks in all, two of each color. Each sock is its own sock.

A set of four is just which socks she holds. The order doesn't matter.

At least one pair means one matching pair, or even two pairs. Both count.

## Solution 1: all minus no-pair

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: count everything, then take away the sets with no pair.

All sets minus no-pair sets leaves the sets with a pair.

First, all sets. Pick socks one at a time: ten choices, then nine, then eight, then seven.

That's five thousand forty.

But each set got counted once for every order.

Four socks can line up in four times three times two times one, twenty-four orders.

So five thousand forty divided by twenty-four is two hundred ten sets in all.

Now the no-pair sets. No pair means four different colors.

Choose four of the five colors. That's the same as choosing the one color to leave out: five ways.

Each chosen color has two socks to pick from. Two times two times two times two is sixteen.

So there are five times sixteen, eighty sets with no pair.

Two hundred ten minus eighty is one hundred thirty.

One hundred thirty sets have a pair. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: count the pairs directly

Here's another way. Let's count the sets with a pair directly, in two groups.

Exactly one pair: five colors for the pair.

Then two different colors from the other four, six ways, and a sock of each, four ways.

Five times six times four is one hundred twenty.

Two pairs: choose two of the five colors. There are ten ways.

One hundred twenty plus ten is one hundred thirty. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? It's a great double-check. But the first way didn't need to split into cases.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A says eighty. That's the sets with NO pair, the group we took away.

Choice E, two hundred ten, is every set, before taking any away.

Choice D, one hundred forty, comes from picking a pair, five ways.

Then any two of the other eight socks: eight times seven over two, twenty-eight ways. Five times twenty-eight is one hundred forty.

But a two-pair set gets counted twice, once for each pair.

Take away the ten extras, and you're back to one hundred thirty.

Choice B, one hundred twenty-five, doesn't come from any correct count. Both our methods give one hundred thirty.

So here's a great habit. For at least one, count the opposite and subtract.

## Six pairs: the answer

Let's check. Count every set: twelve times eleven times ten times nine, divided by twenty-four.

That's four hundred ninety-five.

No pair: choose four of the six colors.

That's the same as leaving out two colors: six times five over two, fifteen ways. Then sixteen ways to pick the socks.

Fifteen times sixteen is two hundred forty sets with no pair.

Four hundred ninety-five minus two hundred forty is two hundred fifty-five!

Check: one pair gives six times ten times four, two hundred forty, and two pairs gives six times five over two, fifteen.

Two hundred fifty-five again!

If you got two hundred fifty-five, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

When a question says at least one, count the opposite.

Count all the sets, subtract the ones with none, and what's left is your answer.
