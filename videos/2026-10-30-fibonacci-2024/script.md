## The question

In a sequence of positive whole numbers, each term is the sum of the two before it.

Term ten is two thousand twenty-four.

What is the largest possible value of the first term plus the second term?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Let's break it down. Once you pick the first two terms, the rest is fixed.

For example, start with one and two. Then come three, five, eight, and so on.

Our start must make term ten exactly two thousand twenty-four.

Out of all those starts, we want the biggest first term plus second term.

## Solution 1: write every term with x and y

Welcome back! Let's call the first term x, and the second term y.

The third term is x plus y. The fourth is x plus two y.

Keep adding the last two terms, and watch the numbers in front grow.

Term ten is twenty-one x plus thirty-four y.

Look at the numbers in front. They're the Fibonacci numbers!

So our equation is twenty-one x plus thirty-four y equals two thousand twenty-four.

We want x plus y. So split thirty-four y into twenty-one y plus thirteen y.

Now twenty-one x plus twenty-one y is twenty-one times x plus y.

Call that sum T, for total. Now twenty-one T plus thirteen y equals two thousand twenty-four.

To make T big, thirteen y should be as small as possible. So we want a small y.

y is at least one, so twenty-one T is at most two thousand eleven.

Twenty-one times ninety-six is too big, so T is at most ninety-five.

Now let's count down from ninety-five. The leftover must be thirteen times y.

T equals ninety-five leaves twenty-nine. That's not a multiple of thirteen.

Each step down adds twenty-one, so next come fifty, seventy-one, ninety-two, and so on.

None of them work, until T equals eighty-four. The leftover is two hundred sixty.

Two hundred sixty is thirteen times twenty. So y is twenty!

Then x is eighty-four minus twenty, which is sixty-four.

Let's check. Start with sixty-four and twenty, and keep adding.

Eighty-four, one hundred four, one hundred eighty-eight, and on up to term ten.

Term ten is two thousand twenty-four! So the answer is eighty-four, Choice C.

## Solution 2: test the choices

Here's a faster way. Notice that x plus y is exactly term three. That's T!

So start the list at term two and term three. That's y, then T.

Build it up the same way. Term ten is thirteen y plus twenty-one T. Same equation!

Biggest first: twenty-one times one hundred five is two thousand two hundred five. Too big!

T equals ninety-four leaves fifty. Fifty is not a multiple of thirteen.

T equals eighty-four leaves two hundred sixty, which is thirteen times twenty.

So y is twenty, and x is sixty-four, which is positive. It works!

When would you pick this way? When a question asks for the largest, test from the top.

## Common traps

Choice B is seventy-one. Keep counting down, and T equals seventy-one works too.

Then x is thirty and y is forty-one. Try small x first, and you might find this one and stop!

But seventy-one is smaller than eighty-four. The next one, fifty-eight, makes x negative.

You could get the others by mixing the two starts. Sixty-four plus forty-one is one hundred five.

Sixty-four plus thirty is ninety-four, and thirty plus twenty is fifty. No mixed pair works here.

So here's a great habit: find every start that works, then compare them.
