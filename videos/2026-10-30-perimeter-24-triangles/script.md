# How Many Triangles Have Perimeter 24? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Three whole-number sides that add up to twenty-four. How many different triangles can you make?

Missing just one or two is the classic slip. I'm Vantee, and we'll find every single one!

## The question

Here's the question.

How many different triangles have whole-number side lengths and a perimeter of twenty-four?

Triangles with the same three side lengths count once. Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

We need three whole numbers that add up to twenty-four. Each one is a side.

But not every three numbers make a triangle. Two, two and twenty can't close up.

And order doesn't matter. Seven, eight, nine is the same triangle as nine, eight, seven.

## Solution 1: list by longest side

Welcome back! First, the triangle rule.

The two shorter sides must add up to MORE than the longest side, or they can't meet.

If they only tie, the triangle squashes flat into a line.

Now here's the trick. The two shorter sides make up the rest of the perimeter.

So the longest side must be shorter than half the perimeter. Half of twenty-four is twelve.

If the longest side is under twelve, the other sides always fit. So that's our only test.

How short can the longest side be? If it were seven, all three sides would be seven or less.

Seven plus seven plus seven is only twenty-one. Too short! So the longest side is at least eight.

So the longest side is eight, nine, ten or eleven. Let's make a list for each one.

Longest side eleven. The other two sides add up to thirteen.

The middle side can't be more than eleven. So we start with two and eleven.

Then three and ten, four and nine, five and eight, six and seven. That's five triangles.

Why stop there? Next is seven and six, which is six and seven again.

Longest side ten. The other two add up to fourteen, so we start with four and ten.

Then five and nine, six and eight, seven and seven. That's four triangles.

Longest side nine. The rest adds up to fifteen: six and nine, then seven and eight. That's two.

Longest side eight. The other two add up to sixteen. Only eight and eight fits. That's one!

Five, plus four, plus two, plus one. That's twelve triangles! The answer is C.

## Solution 2: the gap trick

Here's a clever second way. Measure how far each side is from twelve, half the perimeter. Call it the gap.

Take seven, eight, nine. The gaps are five, four and three.

Three twelves make thirty-six. The sides use twenty-four. So the gaps always add up to twelve.

Every gap is at least one, because every side is less than twelve.

It works backwards too. Gaps that add up to twelve give sides under twelve, so a real triangle.

So we count the ways to split twelve into three whole numbers, each at least one.

A biggest gap of ten gives one way. Nine, one way. Eight, two ways. Seven, two ways.

Six, three ways. Five, two ways. And four, four, four is one more.

One plus one plus two plus two plus three plus two plus one. Twelve again!

When would you pick this one? It's a great double-check on your list.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Ten is what you'd get if you left out the longest side of nine. Those two triangles go missing.

Eleven is what you'd get if you forgot eight, eight, eight. An equilateral triangle still counts!

Thirteen is what you'd get if one triangle is counted twice, in a different order.

And nineteen is just too many. Our full list has only twelve.

So here's a great habit. Pick the longest side first, then list the other two in order.

## Perimeter twenty: the answer

Let's check. Half of twenty is ten. So the longest side is less than ten.

Six plus six plus six is only eighteen. So the longest side is at least seven.

Longest side nine. The other two add up to eleven.

Two and nine, three and eight, four and seven, five and six. That's four.

Longest side eight. The rest is twelve: four and eight, five and seven, six and six. That's three.

Longest side seven. The rest is thirteen. Only six and seven works. That's one.

Notice there's no equilateral this time. Twenty can't split into three equal whole numbers.

Four, plus three, plus one. That's eight triangles! If you found eight, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

The longest side must be shorter than half the perimeter, since the other two must add up to more.

So pick the longest side first, find its limits, then list the other two in order.
