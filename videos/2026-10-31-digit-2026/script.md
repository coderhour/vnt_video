## The question

Here's the question.

The whole numbers are written in order, with no spaces. One, two, three, and so on.

After nine comes one zero, then one one, and the digits keep running together.

What is the two thousand twenty-sixth digit?

## What are we asked?

Each spot holds one digit. Spots ten and eleven hold the one and the zero of ten.

Small numbers use one digit each. Bigger numbers use two, then three.

We need two things: which number covers spot two thousand twenty-six, and which of its digits it is.

## Solution 1: count digits in blocks

Welcome back! Let's count the digits in blocks.

One to nine: nine numbers, one digit each. That's nine digits.

Ten to ninety-nine: that's ninety numbers. Ninety-nine minus ten, plus one.

We add one because we count both ten and ninety-nine.

Each one has two digits, so that's one hundred eighty digits.

So far: nine plus one hundred eighty, one hundred eighty-nine digits.

Spot two thousand twenty-six is still ahead. We've used one hundred eighty-nine digits.

Two thousand twenty-six minus one hundred eighty-nine is one thousand eight hundred thirty-seven.

Now every number has three digits. So divide one thousand eight hundred thirty-seven by three.

That's six hundred twelve, remainder one.

So six hundred twelve full three-digit numbers fit, starting at one hundred.

Careful here! They run from one hundred up to seven hundred eleven.

Six hundred twelve numbers use one thousand eight hundred thirty-six digits.

That reaches spot two thousand twenty-five, the last digit of seven hundred eleven.

The remainder one means one more digit: the first digit of the next number, seven hundred twelve.

The two thousand twenty-sixth digit is seven. That's Choice D!

## Solution 2: jump by hundreds

Here's another way. Every hundred three-digit numbers uses three hundred digits.

Start at one hundred eighty-nine, and jump by three hundreds.

By the end of six hundred ninety-nine, we've used one thousand nine hundred eighty-nine digits.

The next jump would pass two thousand twenty-six. So our digit is in the seven hundreds!

Two thousand twenty-six minus one thousand nine hundred eighty-nine is thirty-seven.

That's twelve threes, plus one.

Twelve numbers, seven hundred through seven hundred eleven. Then one more digit.

It's the first digit of seven hundred twelve. Seven again!

When would you pick this way? When the target is far away. Big jumps first, then small steps.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choice A, one, is the middle digit of seven hundred twelve.

That reads the wrong spot inside the number.

Choice B, two, is the last digit. Same mistake: the remainder one means the FIRST digit.

Choice C, six, is what you'd get if you forgot that three-digit numbers start at one hundred.

Count from zero instead of one hundred, and the next number would be six hundred twelve.

Choice E, eight, can't happen. Our digit is the first digit of a number in the seven hundreds.

So it must be seven.

## 1000th digit: the answer

Let's check. The first one hundred eighty-nine digits are used up again.

One thousand minus one hundred eighty-nine is eight hundred eleven.

Eight hundred eleven divided by three is two hundred seventy, remainder one.

Two hundred seventy numbers run from one hundred to three hundred sixty-nine.

The remainder one is the first digit of the next number, three hundred seventy.

So the digit in spot one thousand is three!

If you got three, brilliant counting!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Count the digits block by block, and subtract what's used up.

Then divide by the digits per number. How many times it goes in finds the number.

The remainder finds the digit inside it.
