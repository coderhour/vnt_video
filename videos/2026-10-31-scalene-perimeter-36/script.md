# Perimeter 36, Three Different Sides: How Many Triangles? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Take a string thirty-six units long, and bend it into a triangle.

How many different shapes can you make?

Watch out: some tries go flat, and some repeat a side!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's count them without missing any.

## The question

Here's the question.

How many triangles have whole-number side lengths and a perimeter of thirty-six,

and have three different side lengths?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

The three sides must add to thirty-six. For example, ten, twelve and fourteen.

All three must be different, so twelve, twelve, twelve doesn't count.

And the same three sides in a different order make the same triangle. We count it once.

## Solution 1: list by the longest side

Welcome back! Let's make an organized list. Write each triangle smallest side first.

Here's the triangle rule. The two shorter sides must add to MORE than the longest side.

The shorter two make thirty-six minus the longest. So the longest side must be less than eighteen.

And the longest side is at least a third of thirty-six. That's twelve.

So the longest side is somewhere from twelve to seventeen.

Longest side seventeen: the other two add to nineteen.

From three and sixteen up to nine and ten. That's seven.

The middle side has to stay below seventeen, and the smallest below the middle.

Longest side sixteen: the other two add to twenty. From five and fifteen up to nine and eleven.

That's five more.

Longest fifteen: seven and fourteen, up to ten and eleven. That's four.

Longest fourteen gives two. Longest thirteen gives just one: eleven, twelve, thirteen.

Longest twelve gives none. Two different sides below twelve add to at most twenty-one.

Seven plus five plus four plus two plus one is nineteen. That's Choice B!

## Solution 2: count all, then subtract

Here's another way. Count every triangle, then take away the ones with a repeated side.

Allow repeats, and the same list grows to twenty-seven triangles in all.

A triangle with a repeat has two equal sides. Call each one x.

So the third side is thirty-six minus two x.

The third side must be more than zero, so x is less than eighteen.

And x plus x must beat the third side, so x is more than nine.

That's x from ten to seventeen: eight triangles.

Twelve, twelve, twelve is one of them, counted just once.

Twenty-seven minus eight is nineteen. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When the leftovers are easier to count than what you want.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choice D, twenty-seven, counts every triangle. It forgets to remove the repeats.

Choice C, twenty, removes the repeats but forgets twelve, twelve, twelve.

That's twenty-seven minus seven.

Choice E, thirty-five, is too many. There are only twenty-seven triangles in all!

Choice A, seventeen, is what you'd get if you skipped a row of the list.

For example, the two with longest side fourteen.

An organized list by longest side keeps every triangle, so we land on nineteen.

## Perimeter 24: the answer

Let's check. The longest side must be less than twelve, and at least eight.

Longest eleven: the other two add to thirteen.

Three and ten, four and nine, five and eight, six and seven. Four!

Longest ten: five and nine, six and eight. That's two.

Longest nine: just seven and eight. And longest eight gives none.

Four plus two plus one is seven triangles!

Check another way: twelve triangles in all, minus five with a repeat. Seven!

If you got seven, terrific work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Fix the longest side. It's at least a third of the perimeter, and less than half.

Then list row by row. Or count them all, and subtract the repeats.
