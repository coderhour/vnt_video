# Which Divisors End in 1? | AMC 10-Style Level 4

## Intro

This number has one hundred five divisors. How many of them end in the digit one?

You don't need to list them. Just watch the LAST digit.

## The question

Here's the question. Take three to the sixth, times seven to the fourth, times thirteen squared.

How many of its positive divisors end in the digit one?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Every divisor looks like three to the a, times seven to the b, times thirteen to the c.

Here, a goes from zero to six, b from zero to four, and c from zero to two.

That's seven times five times three. One hundred five divisors, way too many to list!

Good news: to find a product's last digit, you only multiply the last digits.

## Solution 1: a last-digit grid

Welcome back! Let's look for a pattern. Here are the powers of three.

One, three, nine, twenty-seven, eighty-one. Their last digits go one, three, nine, seven, repeat.

Powers of seven end in one, seven, nine, three, and then one again.

Powers of thirteen end in one, three, nine. Thirteen acts just like three!

Now let's build a grid. Across, a goes from zero to six. Down, c goes from zero to two.

Each of the twenty-one boxes holds the last digit of three to the a, times thirteen to the c.

Let's find the ones. Two in the top row, one in the middle, and two at the bottom. Five in all!

Now bring in the sevens. Each box needs a partner from the powers of seven.

A box ending in one needs a partner ending in one. That's b equals zero, or four. Two ways!

A box ending in three needs a seven, because three times seven is twenty-one.

Nine needs a nine, for eighty-one. And seven needs a three, for twenty-one again.

So each of those boxes gets exactly one partner.

Five boxes end in one, with two ways each. That's ten. The other sixteen boxes add sixteen.

Ten plus sixteen. So the answer is twenty-six! Choice C.

## Solution 2: everything is a power of three

Here's a slicker way. Three cubed is twenty-seven, and it ends in seven.

Seven acts like three cubed, so seven to the b acts like three to the three b.

Thirteen acts like three. So every divisor acts like three to the a plus three b plus c.

A power of three ends in one when its exponent is a multiple of four.

So we need a plus three b plus c to be a multiple of four.

Let's go one b at a time. Here's our grid again, now filled with a plus c.

When b is zero, a plus c must be zero, four, or eight. One, plus three, plus one. Five boxes.

When b is one, three b is three. So a plus c plus three must be four or eight.

That makes a plus c equal to one or five. Two plus three, five boxes.

When b is two, three b is six. So a plus c is two or six. Three plus three, six boxes.

When b is three, three b is nine. So a plus c is three or seven. Three plus two, five boxes.

b equals four works just like b equals zero. Five boxes.

Five plus five plus six plus five plus five. Twenty-six again!

When would you pick this one? When every prime acts like a power of the same number.

## Common traps

Choice A, twenty-four, is what you'd get forgetting one and the number itself. Both end in one!

B, twenty-five, is what you'd get if sevens always ended in one. Five boxes times five choices.

D, twenty-seven, is what you'd get if thirteens ended in one: nine pairs for a and b, three for c.

Choice E, twenty-eight, is just too many. We checked every box, and only twenty-six work.

So here's a great habit. Check each prime's last-digit cycle. Never assume it ends in one.

## New number: the answer

Same grid! Now a goes from zero to five, and c from zero to three. That's twenty-four boxes.

A box gives last digit one when a plus c is zero, four, or eight. One, plus four, plus one. Six boxes.

Each of those gets two partners, b equals zero or four. That's twelve.

Twenty-four minus six leaves eighteen boxes, one partner each. That's eighteen more.

Twelve plus eighteen. The answer is thirty!

## Recap

The big idea: only last digits matter, and last digits repeat in cycles.

Make each prime a power of three, for last digits. Then the exponent must be a multiple of four.
