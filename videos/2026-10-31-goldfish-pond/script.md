# How Many Goldfish Must Go? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

A pond goes from ninety percent goldfish down to eighty percent. How many goldfish leave?

Most kids guess ten. The real answer is much bigger!

Hello, I'm Vantee! Let's go fishing for the answer.

## The question

Here's the question.

A pond has two hundred fish.

Ninety percent of them are goldfish.

How many goldfish must be taken out so that eighty percent of the fish left are goldfish?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Two hundred fish, and ninety percent are goldfish.

We only take out goldfish. The other fish all stay in the pond.

At the end, eighty percent of the fish that are LEFT must be goldfish.

## Solution 1: watch the fish that stay

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: watch the fish that don't change.

Ten percent of two hundred is twenty. So ninety percent is nine times twenty, one hundred eighty goldfish.

That leaves twenty other fish.

Here's the key. We never take out those twenty fish. They stay, no matter what.

At the end, eighty percent are goldfish. So the other fish make up twenty percent.

Twenty percent is the same as one out of five.

So our twenty other fish are one fifth of the pond. The whole pond is five times twenty, one hundred fish.

We started with two hundred fish and end with one hundred. So one hundred goldfish were taken out.

Check: eighty goldfish out of one hundred fish. That's eighty percent!

The answer is one hundred. That's Choice E!

## Solution 2: use a ratio

Here's another way. Think of a ratio.

Eighty percent goldfish and twenty percent others. That's four goldfish for every one other fish.

There are twenty other fish, so there must be four times twenty, eighty goldfish.

We had one hundred eighty goldfish, and we need eighty. Take out one hundred. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When the percents turn into a neat ratio, like four to one.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A, ten, is ninety minus eighty. But that's a difference of percents, not a number of fish!

Here's a quick test. The twenty others must be one fifth of the fish left.

Take out ten, and one hundred ninety are left. One fifth of that is thirty-eight, not twenty.

Choice B, twenty, is the number of other fish, not the number to take out.

One hundred eighty left, so one fifth of that is thirty-six.

Choice C, forty: one hundred sixty left, so one fifth of that is thirty-two.

Choice D, fifty: one hundred fifty left, one fifth is thirty.

Only one hundred works: one hundred left, and one fifth is exactly twenty.

So here's a great habit. Find the group that doesn't change, and build from it.

## Bigger pond: the answer

Let's check. Five percent are not goldfish, and five percent of four hundred is twenty.

Twenty other fish again!

At the end, goldfish are seventy-five percent, so the others are twenty-five percent. That's one quarter.

Twenty is one quarter of the pond, so the pond ends with four times twenty, eighty fish.

Four hundred minus eighty is three hundred twenty goldfish taken out!

So the pond starts with three hundred eighty goldfish.

Check: three hundred eighty goldfish minus three hundred twenty leaves sixty.

Sixty out of eighty is seventy-five percent.

Here's the sneaky part. Going from ninety-five to seventy-five percent emptied most of the pond!

If you got three hundred twenty, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

When only one kind of thing is removed, the other kind stays the same.

Use that fixed group to find the new total. Then subtract.
