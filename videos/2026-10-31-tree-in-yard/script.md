# Where Is the Tree? A Rectangle Puzzle | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

A tree stands on the edge of a yard, exactly as far from one corner as from another.

Where is it? A right triangle is hiding in the yard, and it tells us.

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's find that tree.

## The question

Here's the question.

A rectangular yard A B C D has A B equal to sixteen meters, and B C equal to eight meters.

A tree at point E on side A B

is the same distance from A as from C.

How far is the tree from A, in meters?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? The distance from E to A equals the distance from E to C.

E A runs along the bottom. E C is slanted, and it makes a right triangle with the corner B.

We want E A.

## Solution 1: test the choices

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: test each choice with the Pythagorean theorem.

Triangle E B C has a right angle at B. So E B squared plus eight squared equals E C squared.

And E C must equal E A. So E B squared plus sixty-four must equal E A squared.

Try six. Then E B is ten, and ten squared plus sixty-four is one hundred sixty-four.

But six squared is just thirty-six.

Try eight. Then E B is eight, and eight squared plus sixty-four is one hundred twenty-eight.

Eight squared is only sixty-four.

Try ten. Then E B is six. Six squared plus sixty-four is one hundred.

And ten squared is one hundred. A match!

Triangle E B C has sides six, eight and ten, so E C is ten, just like E A.

The tree is ten meters from A. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: name it x

Here's a way that works even without choices. Call the distance x.

Then E C is also x, and E B is sixteen minus x.

The right triangle says sixteen minus x, squared, plus eight squared, equals x squared.

Sixteen minus x, times sixteen minus x, is two hundred fifty-six, minus sixteen x, minus sixteen x, plus x squared.

Sixteen minus x, squared, opens up to two hundred fifty-six, minus thirty-two x, plus x squared.

Add sixty-four. Now there's an x squared on both sides, so they cancel out.

That leaves three hundred twenty equals thirty-two x. So x is ten. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When there are no choices to test, or the numbers aren't nice.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A, six, is a real length: it's E B, from the tree to B. But we want the distance from A.

Choice B, eight, is the short side B C. And we just saw eight fails the test.

Choices D and E, twelve and fourteen: the length E B is only four, then two, and the numbers don't match.

Eighty versus one hundred forty-four, and sixty-eight versus one hundred ninety-six.

So here's a great habit. When two distances are equal, write one of them a second way, with a right triangle.

## Bigger yard: the answer

Let's call it x again. E B is eighteen minus x, and B C is twelve.

Eighteen minus x, times eighteen minus x, is three hundred twenty-four, minus eighteen x, twice, plus x squared.

Eighteen minus x, squared, is three hundred twenty-four, minus thirty-six x, plus x squared.

Add one hundred forty-four, and the x squared terms cancel.

That leaves four hundred sixty-eight equals thirty-six x. So x is thirteen!

Check: E B is five, and a five, twelve, thirteen triangle is a right triangle. It works!

If you got thirteen meters, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Name the unknown distance. Then write the same distance a second way, with the Pythagorean theorem.

Set the two equal, and solve.
