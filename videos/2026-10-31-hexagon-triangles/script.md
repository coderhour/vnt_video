# How Many Triangles Hide in This Hexagon? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

This hexagon is cut into twenty-four little triangles. But that's not the whole story!

Bigger triangles are hiding in it too. Some of them are sneaky.

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's hunt down every triangle.

## The question

Here's the question.

A regular hexagon has side length two.

It's cut into twenty-four small equilateral triangles.

How many triangles of any size are in the figure?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

Each little triangle has side one. Two small edges make a hexagon side.

We count triangles of every size, as long as their edges are drawn lines.

And watch the direction. Some triangles point up, and some point down.

## Solution 1: count by size

Welcome back! Let's count one size at a time.

Side one is easy. Those are the twenty-four little triangles we were given.

Side two is made of four small triangles.

Six of them fit around the center, like slices of a pizza.

Each slice has a corner right at the center.

But here's a sneaky one. None of its corners is at the center.

The center sits in the middle of one of its sides.

There are six of these too, each pointing to the middle of a hexagon side.

So side two gives six plus six, twelve triangles.

What about side three? Look at the middle of each side of the hexagon.

Join every other middle, and you get one big triangle pointing up.

Join the other three, and you get one pointing down. That's two.

Side four won't fit. Its bottom edge would need one of the long lines through the middle.

But only two rows fit above or below that line.

Twenty-four plus twelve plus two is thirty-eight. That's Choice E!

## Solution 2: count up, then double

Time for a clever shortcut. Count only the triangles that point up.

Twelve of the small ones point up.

Six of the side-two triangles point up, and one of the big ones.

That's twelve plus six plus one, nineteen.

Now turn the hexagon upside down. It looks the same, but every up triangle now points down!

So there are nineteen pointing down too. Two times nineteen is thirty-eight.

When would you pick this way? When the shape looks the same upside down.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choice A, twenty-four, counts only the little triangles.

Choice B, thirty, is what you'd get if you found only the six pizza slices of side two.

Choice C, thirty-two, counts side-two triangles in only one direction.

Choice D, thirty-six, forgets the two big triangles that stretch across the hexagon.

Count every size, in both directions, and you get thirty-eight.

## Big triangle: the answer

Let's check. Side one: the sixteen small ones.

Side two: six point up. And don't miss the sneaky one in the middle, pointing down. That's seven.

Side three: three of them, one in each corner.

Side four: the whole triangle.

Sixteen plus seven plus three plus one is twenty-seven!

If you got twenty-seven, outstanding hunting!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Count the triangles one size at a time, and check both directions, up and down.

If the shape looks the same upside down, count one direction and double it.
