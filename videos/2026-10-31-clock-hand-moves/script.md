# Spin the Hour Hand 1, 2, 3… 20 Hours: Where Does It Stop? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Move a clock's hour hand one hour, then two, then three, all the way up to twenty.

Where does it stop? You won't need to spin it by hand!

## The question

Here's the question.

The hour hand of a clock points to three.

Tom moves it forward one hour, then two hours, then three hours, and so on.

He finishes with a move of twenty hours.

To which number does the hour hand point at the end?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

There are twenty moves, and each one is one hour longer than the last.

Here's a key fact. Twelve hours is one full turn.

The hand lands right back where it started.

We want the number the hand points to at the very end.

## Solution 1: add it all up

Welcome back! Let's add up all the moves first.

We need one plus two plus three, all the way to twenty.

Pair them up. One and twenty make twenty-one. Two and nineteen make twenty-one too.

Twenty numbers make ten pairs. Ten times twenty-one is two hundred ten hours.

Twelve hours is a full turn. Two hundred ten divided by twelve is seventeen, remainder six.

Seventeen full turns bring the hand right back to three. They don't change anything!

Only the six leftover hours matter. Count six more from three.

The hand ends at nine. That's Choice D!

## Solution 2: throw away full turns

Here's another way. Throw away full turns as you go.

One and eleven make twelve. So do two and ten, three and nine, four and eight, and five and seven.

And the move of twelve is a full turn by itself.

From the first twelve moves, only the six is left. So far, the hand goes from three to nine.

Each move from thirteen to twenty is a full turn, plus one to eight extra hours.

One through eight adds to thirty-six. That's three more full turns!

So the last eight moves change nothing. The hand stays at nine.

When would you pick this way? When you'd rather not add big numbers.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choice B, six, is the leftover. It counts from twelve, but the hand started at three!

Choice A, three, says the hand ends where it started. That needs whole turns only.

But two hundred ten isn't a multiple of twelve.

Choices C and E, seven and ten, would need four or seven leftover hours. But the leftover is six.

Start at three, add six, and land on nine.

## Start at 8: the answer

Let's check. Pair them up: one and sixteen make seventeen, and there are eight pairs.

Eight times seventeen is one hundred thirty-six.

One hundred thirty-six divided by twelve is eleven, remainder four.

Count four hours from eight: nine, ten, eleven, twelve.

The hand points to twelve!

Eight plus four lands on the twelve itself.

If you got twelve, fantastic work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Add up all the moves. Full turns of twelve change nothing, so keep only the remainder.

Then count that many from where you started.
