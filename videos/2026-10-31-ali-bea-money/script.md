# Two Swaps, One Total: How Much Money Do They Have? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Two friends, two different money swaps, and two clues.

Can we find how much money they have together? Let's draw it out!

## The question

Here's the question.

If Ali gives Bea twenty percent of his money, they will have the same amount.

If instead Bea gives Ali ten dollars, Ali will have twice as much as Bea.

How many dollars do our two friends, Ali and Bea, have together?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

There are two clues. The word instead means each one starts fresh, from the same amounts again.

We want the total: Ali's money plus Bea's money.

## Solution 1: draw bars

Welcome back! Let's draw a bar model.

Twenty percent is one fifth. So split Ali's money into five equal parts.

Clue one: Ali gives one part to Bea. Now Ali has four parts.

They have the same amount, so Bea now has four parts too.

One of those parts was Ali's gift. So Bea started with three parts.

Clue two, starting fresh: Bea gives Ali ten dollars.

After the swap, Ali has five parts plus ten, and Bea has three parts minus ten.

Twice Bea's money is six parts, minus twenty dollars.

Ali has twice as much, so five parts plus ten equals six parts minus twenty.

Take five parts from both sides. One part minus twenty is ten.

So one part is thirty dollars.

So Ali has five parts, one hundred fifty dollars, and Bea has three parts, ninety dollars.

Together, that's two hundred forty dollars. Choice D!

Let's check clue one. Twenty percent of one hundred fifty is thirty.

Ali keeps one hundred twenty, and Bea ends up with one hundred twenty.

Clue two: Ali ends with one hundred sixty, and Bea drops to eighty.

One hundred sixty is twice eighty. Both clues work!

## Solution 2: use letters

Here's another way. Use letters: a for Ali's dollars and b for Bea's.

Clue one: a minus a fifth of a equals b plus a fifth of a.

So b is a minus two fifths of a. That's three fifths of a.

Clue two: a plus ten equals two times, b minus ten.

That's six fifths of a, minus twenty.

So one fifth of a is thirty. Then a is one hundred fifty, and b is ninety. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When the parts don't come out even, letters still work.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choices A and B, ninety and one hundred fifty, are each person alone.

Choice E, three hundred, doubles Ali's money instead of adding Bea's.

Choice C, two hundred, looks friendly. Let's test it.

The total is eight parts, so one part is twenty-five.

Then Ali has one hundred twenty-five, and Bea has seventy-five.

After Bea gives ten, that's one hundred thirty-five and sixty-five.

Twice sixty-five is only one hundred thirty. Not a match!

Only two hundred forty passes both clues.

## New clues: the answer

Let's check. Twenty-five percent is one quarter, so Ali has four parts.

He gives one part and keeps three.

Bea ends with three parts, so she started with two.

Clue two: four parts plus six equals three times, two parts minus six.

That's six parts minus eighteen. So two parts make twenty-four, and one part is twelve.

Ali has forty-eight, and Bea has twenty-four.

Together, they have seventy-two dollars!

Check: after Bea gives six, it's fifty-four and eighteen. Fifty-four is three times eighteen!

Now clue one: forty-eight minus twelve is thirty-six, and twenty-four plus twelve is thirty-six.

If you got seventy-two, wonderful work!

Turn the percent into equal parts, and turn each clue into a balance.

Find what one part is worth, and everything else falls into place.
