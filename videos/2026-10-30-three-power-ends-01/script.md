# When Does 3^n End in 01? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Three, nine, twenty-seven, eighty-one. Powers of three grow fast!

Can one of them ever end in zero one? Let's find out.

## The question

Here's the question.

What is the smallest positive whole number n, so three to the n ends in zero one?

The choices are four, ten, twenty, forty and one hundred. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what are we really asking?

Keep multiplying by three. We want the last two digits to be zero, then one.

And we want the FIRST power where that happens.

## Solution 1: last digit first

Welcome back! Let's start small, with just the last digit.

Here's a handy fact. A product's last digit depends only on the last digits you multiply.

Three, then nine. Nine times three is twenty-seven, so the last digit is seven.

Seven times three is twenty-one, so one. And one times three is three again.

The last digits go three, nine, seven, one, and then repeat. A loop of four!

So three to the n ends in one only for n equals four, eight, twelve, and so on.

That shrinks our search a lot! Now let's test those powers, in order.

Same trick for two digits: only the last two digits matter. The rest are just hundreds.

Three to the fourth is eighty-one. It ends in one, but not zero one. Not yet!

To jump four more powers, we multiply by eighty-one.

Eighty-one times eighty-one ends in sixty-one. That's three to the eighth.

Sixty-one times eighty-one ends in forty-one. That's three to the twelfth.

Forty-one times eighty-one ends in twenty-one. That's three to the sixteenth.

Notice the pattern? The tens digit drops by two every time.

Twenty-one times eighty-one ends in zero one! That's three to the twentieth.

So the smallest n is twenty. The answer is C!

## Solution 2: just add eighty

Here's a clever second way. Think of eighty-one as eighty plus one.

So times eighty-one means: times eighty, plus one more copy of the number.

Our numbers all end in one. Anything ending in one, times eighty, ends in eighty.

So each time we multiply by eighty-one, we just ADD eighty to the last two digits!

Start at three to the zero, which is one. Every jump of four powers adds another eighty.

We need the eighties to add up to a number ending in zero zero.

Eighty, one hundred sixty, two hundred forty, three hundred twenty, four hundred!

Four hundred is five eighties. Five jumps of four powers is twenty. Three to the twentieth again!

When would you pick this way? When a power ends in one, multiplying turns into adding.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Four stops too soon. Three to the fourth is eighty-one. It ends in one, but not zero one.

Ten might feel like a nice round guess. But ten isn't a multiple of four.

Three to the tenth is fifty-nine thousand forty-nine. It ends in forty-nine, not zero one.

Forty and one hundred are sneaky. Those powers of three really DO end in zero one!

Three to the fortieth is two copies of three to the twentieth, multiplied. So it ends in zero one.

Three to the hundredth is five of them multiplied, so it ends in zero one too.

But the question asks for the SMALLEST. Twenty comes first.

So here's a great habit. Test in order, and stop at the first one that works.

## Powers of nine: the answer

Let's check. Last digit first. Nine, then nine times nine is eighty-one, so one.

Then nine, then one again. A loop of two! So n must be even.

Nine to the second is eighty-one. So each jump of two powers adds eighty again.

Eighty-one, sixty-one, forty-one, twenty-one, zero one. Five jumps again!

Five jumps, and each jump covers two powers. Five times two is ten. So n is ten!

Double-check: nine is three times three. So nine to the tenth is three to the twentieth!

If you found ten, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

First, find the loop of the last digit. It tells you which powers to test.

Then test them in order, keeping just the last two digits.
