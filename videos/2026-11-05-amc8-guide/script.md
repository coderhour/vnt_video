# AMC 8 Explained: Format, Scoring and Test-Day Strategy

## Intro

Every January, thousands of middle schoolers sit down for the same math contest.

Twenty-five questions. Forty minutes. And one rule that most kids miss.

Hi, I'm Vantee. Let's get you ready for the AMC 8!

## What is the AMC 8?

AMC stands for American Mathematics Competitions.

The AMC 8 is for students in grade eight or below.

It's held every January, at your school or another host site.

No calculators allowed. Just you, a pencil, and scratch paper.

The questions start friendly, and get harder as you go.

## Format and scoring

There are twenty-five multiple choice questions, each with five choices, A to E.

You get forty minutes. That's about ninety-six seconds per question.

Each right answer earns one point. The top score is twenty-five.

Here's the rule most kids miss: a wrong answer costs you NOTHING.

A blank also scores zero. So a blank can never beat a guess.

A wild guess has a one in five chance. Cross out two choices, and it's one in three.

The top five percent of scorers make the Honor Roll.

The top one percent make the Distinguished Honor Roll.

## Test-day strategy

Strategy one: pace yourself.

The first ten questions are usually quicker. Aim to finish them in about ten minutes.

That leaves thirty minutes for the last fifteen, where the hard ones live.

Stuck for more than two minutes? Circle it in your booklet, move on, and come back later.

Strategy two: read the question twice.

Circle what it asks for. Is it the area, or the perimeter? Feet, or inches?

Many wrong choices are the right answer to a different question.

Strategy three: use the answer choices.

Estimate first. Choices far from your estimate can go.

You can also plug a choice back into the question, and check it.

Strategy four: never leave a question blank.

With two minutes left, fill in every blank with your best guess.

## Try one

Let's try one, AMC 8-style. What is one hundred ninety-eight times two hundred two?

Choice A: thirty-nine thousand nine hundred ninety-six. B: forty thousand four.

Choice C: thirty-nine thousand two hundred four.

Choices D and E: forty thousand, and four thousand.

No need to multiply it all out. Estimate first: two hundred times two hundred is forty thousand.

So four thousand is way off. Cross it out.

Now the last digit. Eight times two is sixteen, so the answer ends in six.

Only one choice ends in six: thirty-nine thousand nine hundred ninety-six.

Check: two hundred minus two, times two hundred plus two, is forty thousand minus four. It works!

## How to prepare

How do you get ready? Practice with real past AMC 8 problems.

Every few weeks, do a full set of twenty-five, with a forty-minute timer.

Then review every miss. Find the idea, not just the answer.

Learn the big topics: counting, number puzzles, geometry, and early algebra.

And try Vantee's AMC 8-style videos, with every solution and trap explained.

## Recap

So: twenty-five questions, forty minutes, and no penalty for guessing.

Pace yourself, read twice, use the choices, and answer every question.
