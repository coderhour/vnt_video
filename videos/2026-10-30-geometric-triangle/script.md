## The question

Here's the question.

A triangle has three different whole-number side lengths.

The sides form a geometric sequence.

The longest side is two thousand twenty-five. Fun fact: that's forty-five squared!

How many such triangles are there?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

Geometric means times the same number each step. Like four, six, nine: times three over two.

We need three different whole numbers, and the longest one is fixed.

The triangle rule: the two short sides must add up to MORE than the longest side.

## Solution 1: use the ratio

Welcome back! Let's use the ratio.

Each step, we multiply by one fraction, p over q, in lowest terms. It's less than one.

One step gives the middle side. Step two gives the shortest side.

Look at the shortest side. The bottom of the fraction gets squared!

Because p and q share no factors, q squared must divide the longest side.

So which squares divide forty-five squared?

Split it into primes. There are four threes and two fives.

A square uses primes in pairs. So q has at most two threes and one five.

That leaves one, three, five, nine, fifteen, or forty-five. Each one divides forty-five!

So we can always rewrite the fraction over forty-five, even if it's not in lowest terms.

For example, two over three is the same as thirty over forty-five.

Let's call the new top m. So the fraction is m over forty-five.

Now the sides get really simple.

The middle side is forty-five squared, times m over forty-five. That's forty-five times m.

Do it again, and the shortest side is m squared.

The fraction is less than one, so m goes from one to forty-four. That's forty-four sequences to test.

Now let's build each one, and see if it closes.

With m equal to one, the short sides are one and forty-five. They can't reach!

As m grows, both short sides grow too.

At twenty-seven, they add up to just under the longest side. So close!

At twenty-eight, they add up to just over the longest side. It closes!

Bigger m makes both short sides even bigger. So every m after that works too.

So the good values of m go from twenty-eight all the way up to forty-four.

To count them, take forty-four minus twenty-eight, and add one for the first.

That's seventeen triangles, so the answer is Choice B.

## Solution 2: the middle-side trick

Here's a faster way. In a geometric sequence, the middle term squared equals its two neighbors multiplied.

Check: six squared is thirty-six, and four times nine is thirty-six too!

So the middle side squared equals the shortest side, times forty-five squared.

Divide both sides by forty-five squared. The shortest side is the middle side over forty-five, all squared.

Three over two, squared, is nine over four. A fraction in lowest terms stays a fraction when squared.

So the middle side over forty-five must be a whole number.

So the middle side is forty-five times m. And the shortest side is m squared!

Same sides as before! The triangle rule finishes the job.

When would you pick this way? When you spot a middle term, it saves a few steps.

## Common traps

Now let's check the wrong answers.

Choice E, forty-four, counts every m. It forgets the triangle rule!

Choice D, twenty-seven, counts the m values that FAIL. That's backwards.

Choice A, sixteen, takes forty-four minus twenty-eight, and forgets to add one.

Choice C, eighteen, also counts m equal to forty-five. But then all three sides are equal!

Two great habits: check the triangle rule, and count both ends.

## Your turn: the answer

Let's check. One hundred forty-four is four twos and two threes.

So q is one, two, three, four, six, or twelve. Each one divides twelve!

So the fraction is m over twelve. It's less than one, so m stops at eleven.

When m is seven: forty-nine plus eighty-four is one hundred thirty-three. Too small!

With m equal to eight: sixty-four plus ninety-six is one hundred sixty. It closes!

So the good values of m are eight to eleven. That's four triangles!

## Recap

In a geometric sequence, the middle term squared equals its two neighbors multiplied.

That tells you which sides can be whole. Then the triangle rule trims the list.
