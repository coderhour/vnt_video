# Where Do the Lines Cross? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Two lines cross inside a triangle, and one piece is shaded.

It's tempting to guess half the triangle. But the real answer is sneakier.

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's crack it together.

## The question

Here's the question. Triangle A B C has area sixty.

D is the midpoint of B C.

E is on A C, and A E to E C is two to one.

Segments A D and B E meet at point P. What is the area of the shaded triangle A B P?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we really know?

D cuts B C into two equal halves. E cuts A C into three equal parts: two near A, one near C.

The real puzzle is where P sits on A D. Once we know that, we're nearly done.

## Solution 1: same-height triangles

Welcome back! Let's start with one handy rule.

Two triangles with the same corner, and bases on the same line, have the same height.

So their areas compare just like their bases.

D is the midpoint, so triangles A B D and A D C each get half of sixty. That's thirty each.

Now draw one more line, from C to P. That cuts the triangle into five pieces.

Look at B P D and C P D. Same corner P, equal bases. So they're equal. Call each one x.

Now A P E and C P E. Same corner P, and A E is double the length of E C.

So A P E is twice as big. Call C P E y, and A P E two y.

Time for two equations. Triangle A D C is thirty, and its pieces are x, y and two y.

So x plus three y equals thirty.

Next, triangle B E C. It shares corner B with the big triangle, and E C is one third of A C.

So B E C is one third of sixty, which is twenty. Its pieces are x, x and y.

So two x plus y equals twenty.

Let's double the first equation. Two x plus six y equals sixty.

The other one says two x plus y equals twenty. The difference is five y, and that's forty.

So y is eight. Then two x plus eight is twenty, so x is six.

A B D is thirty, and B P D is six. So A B P is thirty minus six: twenty-four! That's C.

Check: A B P and A P E make A B E. Twenty-four plus sixteen is forty, two thirds of sixty.

## Solution 2: a parallel line

Here's a second way, with one clever extra line.

Here's the rule we need. A line parallel to one side of a triangle cuts the other two sides in the same ratio.

From D, draw a line parallel to B E. It meets A C at a new point, F.

D is halfway along C B. So the parallel line lands halfway along C E. F is the midpoint of E C.

Now count steps along A C. E C is two steps, so A E is four steps, and E F is one.

In triangle A D F, P E is parallel to D F. So A P to P D matches A E to E F: four to one.

So A P is four fifths of A D. That makes A B P four fifths of A B D.

Four fifths of thirty is twenty-four. Same answer! It's C.

When would you pick this way? When you need a ratio along a line, fast, with no equations.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Thirty is triangle A B D, half the whole. Too big, because A B P is only part of it.

Forty is triangle A B E, two thirds of the whole. Also too big.

Twenty is what you'd get if you copied two to one onto A D. Two thirds of thirty is twenty.

But two to one belongs to line A C. Line A D has its own split: four to one.

Fifteen is what you'd get if P were the midpoint of A D. Half of thirty is fifteen.

So here's a great habit. Every line gets its own ratio. Find it before you use it.

## New triangle: the answer

Let's use the parallel line. D F is parallel to B E, so F is still the midpoint of E C.

E C is two steps, so A E is six steps, and E F is one. So A P is six sevenths of A D.

Triangle A B D is half of eighty-four, which is forty-two.

Six sevenths of forty-two is thirty-six!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

When two lines cross inside a triangle, find how the crossing point splits each line.

Use same-height triangles, or a parallel line. Then take that fraction of a bigger triangle.
