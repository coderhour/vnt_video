# Multiples of 4, 6 or 9: Don't Count Twice! | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

How many numbers up to one thousand are multiples of four, six or nine?

It's tempting to count each one and add. But some numbers sneak in twice!

## Solution 1: a Venn diagram

Welcome back! First, let's count the multiples of each number on its own.

One thousand divided by four is two hundred fifty. That's two hundred fifty multiples of four.

One thousand divided by six is one hundred sixty-six, with four left over.

One thousand divided by nine is one hundred eleven, with one left over.

Adding them gives five hundred twenty-seven. But wait, some numbers got counted twice!

Twelve is a multiple of four AND six. So we counted it twice.

Where do four and six meet? Four, eight, twelve. And six, twelve. They first meet at twelve.

So the overlap is the multiples of twelve. Twenty-four would miss twelve and thirty-six!

Six and nine first meet at eighteen.

Four and nine share no factor except one. So they first meet at four times nine, thirty-six.

Now divide by twelve, eighteen and thirty-six. We get eighty-three, fifty-five and twenty-seven.

Eighty-three plus fifty-five plus twenty-seven is one hundred sixty-five. Let's take those away.

Five hundred twenty-seven minus one hundred sixty-five,

That is three hundred sixty-two. Are we done?

Not yet! Think about thirty-six. It's a multiple of four, six AND nine.

We counted it three times, then took it away three times. Now it's not counted at all!

All three first meet at thirty-six. So we add back those twenty-seven numbers, once.

Three hundred sixty-two plus twenty-seven,

That makes three hundred eighty-nine. The answer is C!

Here's something cool. Look at the slice for four and nine, without six.

A multiple of four and nine is a multiple of thirty-six. And thirty-six is a multiple of six!

So that slice is empty. That's why the twenty-seven we took away came right back.

## Solution 2: build it up

Now for a second way. Let's build the list one rule at a time.

First, the multiples of four or six.

Two hundred fifty plus one hundred sixty-six, minus eighty-three multiples of twelve.

That's three hundred thirty-three so far. Now, which multiples of nine are NEW?

An even multiple of nine is a multiple of eighteen. So it's a multiple of six, already counted.

Multiples of four and six are all even. So every ODD multiple of nine is new.

There are one hundred eleven multiples of nine, and fifty-five are even. So fifty-six are odd.

Three hundred thirty-three plus fifty-six is three hundred eighty-nine. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When one overlap hides inside another, it saves steps.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Five hundred twenty-seven adds the three counts, but never takes away the overlaps.

Three hundred sixty-two takes away the overlaps, but forgets to add the thirty-sixes back.

Three hundred eighty-eight is what you'd get if you left out one thousand, a multiple of four!

You'd get four hundred sixteen if you skipped four and nine, but added the thirty-sixes back.

Four and nine share no factor, but they still meet at thirty-six, seventy-two and more.

So here's a great habit. For each overlap, find the FIRST number where the two rules meet.

## Four, six or ten: the answer

Let's check. Three hundred divided by four, six and ten gives seventy-five, fifty and thirty.

Adding those gives one hundred fifty-five.

The pairs first meet at twelve, twenty and thirty. That's twenty-five, fifteen and ten numbers.

Take away those fifty. All three meet at sixty, and three hundred divided by sixty is five.

So one hundred fifty-five minus fifty, plus five back,

That makes one hundred ten! Amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea. Add the three counts. Take away each pair's overlap.

Then add back the overlap of all three.

For each overlap, use the smallest number they all go into. That's twelve, not twenty-four!
