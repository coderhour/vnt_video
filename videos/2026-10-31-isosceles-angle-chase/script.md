# Two Isosceles Triangles, One Mystery Angle | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

We know just one angle, twenty-four degrees. Can we find the angle at the top?

The secret is hiding in two isosceles triangles.

## The question

Here's the question.

In triangle A B C, side A B equals side A C.

Point D is on side A C, so that B D equals B C.

Angle A B D is twenty-four degrees.

What is the measure of angle B A C, the angle at the top?

Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

So we have two isosceles triangles. The big one, A B C, has two equal sides from A.

The small one, B D C, has two equal sides from B.

Our goal is the top angle, at A.

## Solution 1: chase with a letter

Welcome back! Let's name what we don't know. Call the top angle a.

Side A B equals side A C, so the angles at B and C are equal. Those are the base angles.

The three angles add to one hundred eighty, so each base angle is ninety minus half of a.

Now the small triangle. B D equals B C, so the angles at D and C match. Angle B D C equals angle C.

So angle D B C is one hundred eighty, minus angle C, minus angle C.

Two base angles add to one hundred eighty minus a. So angle D B C is just a!

Look at that. The little angle at B is the same as the top angle.

Angle A B C is made of two parts: twenty-four degrees, plus a.

But angle A B C is also a base angle. So twenty-four plus a equals ninety minus half of a.

Add half of a to both sides, and take away twenty-four. One and a half a equals sixty-six.

So a is forty-four degrees. That's Choice E!

Check: each base angle is sixty-eight. And sixty-eight minus forty-four is twenty-four. It works!

## Solution 2: the outside angle

Here's another way, with no fractions. Look at triangle A B D, on the left.

An outside angle of a triangle equals the two inside angles far from it.

So angle B D C equals a plus twenty-four.

Angle C matches angle B D C, so it's a plus twenty-four too. And so is angle A B C.

The big triangle adds up: three a plus forty-eight is one hundred eighty.

So three a is one hundred thirty-two, and a is forty-four.

When would you pick this way? When you spot an outside angle. It saves a step.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Choice B, twenty-four, is the angle we were given.

It's not the one we were asked for.

Here's a quick test for any choice.

Find the base angle, then subtract the top angle. You should get twenty-four.

Try twenty-four on top.

The base angle is seventy-eight, and seventy-eight minus twenty-four is fifty-four. Too big.

Choice D, thirty-six, comes from a famous triangle

with angles thirty-six, seventy-two and seventy-two. But here it gives thirty-six, not twenty-four.

Choices A and C, twenty-two and thirty-four, give fifty-seven and thirty-nine. Neither works.

Only forty-four gives exactly twenty-four.

## New angle: the answer

Let's check. The outside angle at D is a plus thirty.

So angle C and angle A B C are both a plus thirty.

The angles add up: three a plus sixty is one hundred eighty. So three a is one hundred twenty.

The top angle is forty degrees!

Check: the base angles are seventy. Seventy minus forty is thirty. Yes!
