# Goats and Growing Grass | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Ten goats can eat a whole field of grass in twenty days.

Add just five more goats, and they finish in ten days. Half the time!

So how do five more goats save ten whole days? Hi, I'm Vantee. Let's find out together.

## The question

Here's the question. A field starts with some grass, and new grass grows at a steady rate.

Ten goats can eat all the grass in twenty days. Fifteen goats can eat it all in ten days.

All goats eat at the same rate. How many days would twenty-five goats take?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Let's break it down. The grass keeps growing while the goats eat.

So the goats eat the starting grass, PLUS all the new grass that grows.

We don't know the starting amount, or how fast it grows. That's two unknowns!

Here's a handy unit. One goat-day is the grass that one goat eats in one day.

Our goal: the number of days for twenty-five goats.

## Solution 1: count goat-days

Welcome back! Let's count everything in goat-days.

Ten goats for twenty days eat ten times twenty, which is two hundred goat-days.

Fifteen goats for ten days eat fifteen times ten, which is one hundred fifty goat-days.

Both cases start with the same grass. So why did the first goats eat more?

Because their field had ten more days to grow!

Those ten extra days grew two hundred minus one hundred fifty, which is fifty goat-days.

Fifty goat-days in ten days is five goat-days each day. Enough to feed five goats!

In twenty days, the field grows twenty times five, which is one hundred goat-days.

So the starting grass is two hundred minus one hundred, which is one hundred goat-days.

Check the fifteen goats: one hundred, plus ten days times five, is one hundred fifty. It matches!

Now let's use twenty-five goats. Each day, they eat twenty-five goat-days.

But five goat-days grow back. So the pile shrinks by twenty-five minus five, which is twenty a day.

One hundred goat-days, at twenty a day, lasts one hundred over twenty, which is five days.

Check: twenty-five times five is one hundred twenty-five. That's one hundred, plus twenty-five more.

The answer is five days. That's choice B!

## Solution 2: helper goats

Here's a clever way to picture it. Let's split the goats into two teams.

The helpers eat only the new grass, as fast as it grows. The rest eat the starting pile.

How many helpers? Let's guess and check. The pile must come out the same in both cases.

No helpers: ten times twenty is two hundred, but fifteen times ten is one hundred fifty.

Each helper takes twenty off the first pile, but only ten off the second. So the gap shrinks by ten.

The gap was fifty, so five helpers close it. Both piles come out at one hundred goat-days!

Now twenty-five goats. Five are helpers, and twenty munch the pile.

Twenty goats finish one hundred goat-days in one hundred over twenty: five days. Same answer!

Pick this way when you like to draw. The helper goats turn a growing field into a pile that stays put.

## Common traps

Now the traps. Most of them forget that the grass keeps growing.

Four is one hundred over twenty-five. It forgets the new grass that grows while the goats eat.

Six is one hundred fifty over twenty-five, and eight is two hundred over twenty-five.

Those totals hold ten or twenty days of growth, but twenty-five goats only take five days.

Ten is two hundred over twenty. It treats the first total as the starting pile.

Remember: the goats eat the starting grass PLUS everything that grows.

## Sheep: the answer

Welcome back! Eight sheep for thirty days eat two hundred forty sheep-days.

Twelve sheep for fifteen days eat one hundred eighty sheep-days.

Fifteen extra days grew sixty sheep-days. That's four sheep-days each day.

Thirty days of growth is thirty times four, which is one hundred twenty sheep-days.

Two hundred forty minus one hundred twenty leaves one hundred twenty to start.

Twenty-four sheep: four are helpers, and twenty munch the pile.

One hundred twenty over twenty is six. The sheep need six days!

## Recap

Let's recap. When the food keeps growing, count in animal-days.

Two cases give you the daily growth, and then the starting pile.

Helpers match the growth, and everyone else eats the starting pile.
