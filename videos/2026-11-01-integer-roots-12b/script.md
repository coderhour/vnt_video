For how many integers b are both roots of x squared plus b x plus twelve b equals zero integers?

Here are the five choices: eight, fifteen, sixteen, thirty and thirty-two.

## What are we asked?

An integer is a whole number: positive, negative or zero. b and both roots must be integers.

We're counting values of b, not pairs of roots.

## Solution 1: sum and product

Welcome back! Call the roots r and s. Then the left side is x minus r, times x minus s.

Multiplying out gives x squared, minus the sum of the roots times x, plus their product.

Match it up! First, b is the negative of the sum: b equals negative r minus s.

Second, twelve b is the product: twelve b equals r times s.

Now let's get rid of b. We know r s equals twelve b, which is negative twelve r minus twelve s.

Move it all to one side: r s plus twelve r plus twelve s equals zero.

Here's the clever step. Think of r plus twelve, times s plus twelve, as a grid with four parts.

The parts are r s, twelve r, twelve s, and twelve times twelve: one hundred forty-four.

Our equation already has the first three parts. So add one hundred forty-four to both sides.

Now it factors! r plus twelve, times s plus twelve, equals one hundred forty-four.

Let's list the factor pairs. One times one hundred forty-four, two times seventy-two.

Three times forty-eight, four times thirty-six, six times twenty-four.

Eight times eighteen, nine times sixteen, and twelve times twelve. That's eight pairs.

Swapping a pair just swaps r and s. Same equation, so the same value of b.

But wait! Two negatives also multiply to a positive. So there are eight negative pairs too.

Each root is its factor minus twelve. So b is twenty-four minus the sum of the pair.

Nine and sixteen add to twenty-five, so b is negative one. That's our opening example!

Twelve and twelve make both roots zero, so b is zero. That counts too!

Different pairs have different sums, so each pair gives its own value of b.

Eight positive pairs plus eight negative pairs is sixteen. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: chase one root

Here's another way: chase one root. Plug r in: r squared plus b r plus twelve b equals zero.

Group the b terms: b times r plus twelve equals negative r squared.

Let d be r plus twelve, so r is d minus twelve.

Then r squared is d squared, minus twenty-four d, plus one hundred forty-four.

Divide by d, and b equals twenty-four, minus d, minus one hundred forty-four over d.

So d must divide one hundred forty-four. That gives thirty choices, positive and negative.

The other root, s, is negative b minus r. So it's an integer too.

d is sixteen for the root four, and nine for the root negative three. Both give b negative one.

So the d values pair up. Only twelve and negative twelve pair with themselves.

The other twenty-eight make fourteen pairs. Fourteen plus two is sixteen!

When would you pick this way? If you forget sum and product. One root is enough.

## Common traps

Choice A, eight, counts only the positive pairs. It forgets the eight negative ones.

Choice B, fifteen, treats each positive divisor as its own b. But nine and sixteen share one b.

Choice D, thirty, counts all thirty divisors. Most values of b get counted twice, once per root.

Choice E, thirty-two, is what you'd get by doubling sixteen, as if swapping roots made a new b.

## x² + bx + 6b: the answer

Same steps! The sum is negative b, the product is six b.

Get rid of b, and we get r times s, plus six times r, plus six times s, equals zero.

Add thirty-six to both sides: r plus six, times s plus six, equals thirty-six.

Pairs that make thirty-six: one and thirty-six, two and eighteen, three and twelve.

Four and nine, and six and six. That's five pairs, plus five negative pairs.

Five plus five gives ten values of b!

Check: four and nine give roots negative two and three. Sum one, so b is negative one.

If you got ten, amazing work!
