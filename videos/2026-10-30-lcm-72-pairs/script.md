# How Many Pairs Have LCM 72? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Two numbers have a least common multiple of seventy-two. How many such pairs are there?

Most kids find a few pairs, then guess. There are more than you would think!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's count them all together.

## The question

Here's the question.

How many ordered pairs of positive whole numbers

have least common multiple seventy-two?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what's a least common multiple? It's the smallest number that both a and b go into.

Take eight and nine. The smallest number they both go into is seventy-two. So that pair counts!

Ordered means order matters. Eight, nine and nine, eight are two DIFFERENT pairs.

## Solution 1: a table of partners

Welcome back! Since a and b both go into seventy-two, each one is a divisor of seventy-two.

Seventy-two is eight times nine. That's two times two times two, times three times three.

Let's put all twelve divisors in a table. Powers of two go across, and powers of three go down.

Here's the trick. Seventy-two has three twos. One number must bring all three: a multiple of eight.

Seventy-two has two threes, too. So one number must bring both: a multiple of nine.

Say a is twelve. It's not a multiple of eight or nine. So b must be both. Only seventy-two works!

Say a is eight. Then b only needs to be a multiple of nine.

That's nine, eighteen, thirty-six or seventy-two. That makes four partners!

If a is seventy-two, it brings everything. So b can be any of the twelve divisors!

Twelve and these five others aren't multiples of eight or nine. One partner each.

Eight and twenty-four are multiples of eight, but not nine. Four partners each.

Nine, eighteen and thirty-six need eight, twenty-four or seventy-two. Three partners each.

Now add. The top row is one plus one plus one plus four, which is seven. So is the middle row.

The bottom row is three plus three plus three plus twelve. That's twenty-one.

Seven plus seven plus twenty-one is thirty-five! The answer is D.

## Solution 2: one prime at a time

Now for a second way. Let's look at the twos and the threes separately.

Take eight and eighteen. Eight has three twos and no threes. Eighteen has one two and two threes.

Their least common multiple keeps the most of each: three twos and two threes. That's seventy-two!

Let's make a grid for the twos. Across is how many twos a has: zero, one, two or three.

Down is how many twos b has. Four by four makes sixteen squares.

The most twos must be three. So at least one of a and b has three twos.

That's the last column and the bottom row. Four plus four, but the corner is in both. Seven!

Let's double-check. The top-left three by three block has no three. Sixteen minus nine is seven.

Now the threes. Each of a and b has zero, one or two threes. That's a three by three grid.

At least one needs two threes. Three plus three, minus the corner, is five. Or nine minus four!

Any way for the twos can go with any way for the threes. Like seven shirts and five pairs of pants.

Seven times five is thirty-five. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When the number is big, and the table would get huge.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Twelve counts the divisors of seventy-two. But we're counting pairs, not single numbers.

Eighteen is what you'd get if order didn't matter. Then eight, nine and nine, eight are one pair.

Every pair has a twin, except seventy-two, seventy-two. Thirty-four over two, plus one, is eighteen.

Twenty-four is what you'd get if you doubled the twelve divisors. But eight alone has four partners!

Seventy is what you'd get if you doubled thirty-five to handle order. But we already counted both orders.

So here's a great habit. Before you count, ask: does order matter? Here, it does!

## LCM 48: the answer

Let's check. Forty-eight is sixteen times three. That's four twos and one three.

For the twos, each of a and b has zero to four. That's a five by five grid, twenty-five squares.

The misses have fewer than four twos: a four by four block. Twenty-five minus sixteen is nine.

For the threes, it's zero or one. Two by two is four, minus one miss. That's three.

Nine times three is twenty-seven! If you found it, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea. For each prime, the least common multiple keeps the bigger of the two powers.

So make a grid of powers for each prime, count the good squares, then multiply.
