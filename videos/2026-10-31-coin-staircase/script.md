# Will She Land on Step 5? A Coin-Flip Staircase | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Pia climbs a staircase, flipping a coin before every move. Will she land on step five?

Sometimes she does. Sometimes she jumps right over it!

Hello, I'm Vantee! Let's work out the chance.

## The question

Here's the question.

Pia climbs a long staircase. Before each move, she flips a fair coin.

On heads, she goes up one step.

On tails, she goes up two steps.

What is the probability that she stands on step five?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Pia starts on the ground, below step one.

Each flip is a fair coin: heads, one step, or tails, two steps.

We want the chance that she actually stands on step five at some point, instead of jumping over it.

## Solution 1: make a list

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: make a list of every way to reach step five.

All ones: one, one, one, one, one.

That's five flips, so the chance is one over two, five times. One over thirty-two.

One two and three ones. The two can go in four places, and each takes four flips.

Each one is one over sixteen.

Two twos and one one. The one can go in three places, three flips each. Each one is one over eight.

Now add them up: one over thirty-two, plus four times one over sixteen, plus three times one over eight.

Over thirty-two, that's one, plus eight, plus twelve.

One plus eight plus twelve is twenty-one. So the chance is twenty-one over thirty-two.

The answer is twenty-one over thirty-two. That's Choice D!

## Solution 2: climb the ladder

Here's a faster way: work up the stairs, one step at a time.

To stand on a step, you come from one below with heads, or from two below with tails. Each move has a one over two chance.

So the chance is half of one step's chance, plus half of the other's. That's their average.

She's on the ground for sure, that's one. Step one needs heads first: one over two.

Step two is the average of one over two and one. That's three over four.

Step three: the average of three over four and one over two is five over eight.

Step four: the average of five over eight and three over four is eleven over sixteen.

Step five: five over eight is ten over sixteen.

The average of eleven over sixteen and ten over sixteen is twenty-one over thirty-two.

When would you pick this way? When the list gets long, this way never gets harder.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A, one over two, feels like land or skip, half and half.

But she only skips step five from step four with tails, so landing is more likely.

Choice B, eight over thirteen, is sneaky.

Eight climbs land on step five, and five climbs jump from four to six.

That's thirteen climbs. But they aren't equally likely! A short climb with fewer flips is more likely.

Choices C and E, five over eight and eleven over sixteen, are the chances for steps three and four.

Both stopped too early!

So here's a great habit. With random steps, build each spot's chance from the spots before it.

## Step six: the answer

Let's climb one more step on our ladder. We know steps four and five.

Eleven over sixteen is twenty-two over thirty-two.

The average of that and twenty-one over thirty-two is forty-three over sixty-four!

Check with a trick. She misses step six only if she's on step five and flips tails.

That's twenty-one over sixty-four.

One minus twenty-one over sixty-four is forty-three over sixty-four. It matches!

If you got forty-three over sixty-four, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

With random steps, the chance of landing on a spot comes from the spots that lead to it.

Build the chances up one step at a time, then read off the step you need.
