# Where Does the Midpoint Chase End? | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Two points on a number line. Then a chase begins.

Each new point lands halfway between the last two. Where does it end up?

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's follow the chase together.

## The question

Here's the question.

Point P one is at zero, and point P two is at sixty-four, on a number line.

Each later point is the midpoint of the two points before it.

So P three is at thirty-two, and P four is at forty-eight.

Where does P ten land?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we really know?

We start with two points. Each new point is the midpoint of the newest two.

We need P ten. That's eight more midpoints after P two.

The choices use decimals. Here's a key for halves, quarters and eighths.

## Solution 1: follow the jumps

Welcome back! Let's make a list, one point at a time, and watch the jumps.

From zero to sixty-four is a jump of sixty-four, to the right.

P three is the midpoint, thirty-two. That's a jump of thirty-two, back to the left.

P four is halfway between thirty-two and sixty-four. That's forty-eight, sixteen to the right.

Notice the pattern? Each jump is half the one before, and it flips direction.

Why? The new point sits halfway along the last jump. So it goes back, half as far.

So the next jumps are eight left, four right, two left, and one right.

That puts P five at forty, P six at forty-four, P seven at forty-two, and P eight at forty-three.

See how the points squeeze closer and closer? They close in on one spot.

Next, a jump of one half to the left. P nine is forty-two and a half.

Last, a jump of one quarter to the right.

P ten is forty-two and three quarters! The answer is C.

## Solution 2: pair up the jumps

Here's a clever second way. Start at zero, and add up all nine jumps.

Right jumps are plus, left jumps are minus. Sixty-four, minus thirty-two, plus sixteen, and so on.

Let's pair them up. Sixty-four minus thirty-two is thirty-two.

Sixteen minus eight is eight. Four minus two is two.

One minus one half is one half. The last jump, one quarter, is on its own.

Look, each pair is a quarter of the pair before: thirty-two, eight, two, one half.

Thirty-two plus eight plus two is forty-two.

One half plus one quarter is three quarters. So it's forty-two and three quarters again!

When would you pick this way? When there are lots of steps, pairs keep it tidy.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a real point in the chase!

Forty-two and a half is P nine. That answer is one step early.

Forty-three is P eight. You'd get it if you stopped before the jumps turned into fractions.

Forty-four is P six. That stops far too early.

Next, forty-two and five over eight. That is P eleven, one step late.

You'd get it if you called the first point P zero. Then your tenth point is really P eleven.

So here's a great habit. Write the point number next to every point as you go.

## New chase: the answer

Let's check. The jumps start at thirty-two, then halve and flip each time.

Thirty-two right, sixteen left, eight right, four left, two right, one left.

Then a half to the right, and a quarter to the left. That's eight jumps, which takes us to P nine.

Pair them up. Thirty-two minus sixteen is sixteen. Eight minus four is four.

Two minus one is one. One half minus one quarter is one quarter.

Sixteen plus four plus one is twenty-one. Then add one quarter.

So P nine is twenty-one and a quarter! If you found that, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

When each new point is a midpoint, each new jump is half the one before, and flips direction.

So list the points one at a time, and watch the jumps. The points close in on one value.
