# Keep the Girls Apart | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Shuffle eight kids into a line. Will two of the girls end up side by side?

Here's a surprise. More often than not, they do!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's find the exact chance, together.

## The question

Here's the question.

Five boys and three girls stand in a row, in a random order. Every order is equally likely.

What is the probability that no two girls stand next to each other?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Let's picture eight spots in a row, numbered one to eight.

This line is good. There's at least one boy between any two girls.

This line is bad. Two girls are standing side by side.

So we want the fraction of all the lines that are good.

## Solution 1: just the girls' spots

Welcome back! Here's a trick. We only care WHERE the girls stand.

Say the girls take spots one, four, and seven.

The girls can fill those spots in three, times two, times one. That's six orders.

The boys fill the other five spots in five, times four, times three, times two, times one.

That's one hundred twenty orders.

Six times one hundred twenty is seven hundred twenty. It's the same for EVERY three spots!

So each set of three spots is equally likely. We can count spots, not names.

How many ways can we pick three spots out of eight?

Pick one spot, then another, then another. Eight, times seven, times six.

That's three hundred thirty-six. But each set of spots got picked in six different orders.

So divide by six. There are fifty-six sets of spots in all.

Now let's list the good sets, sorted by where the first girl stands.

First girl in spot one. The second girl skips spot two, so she can stand in three.

Then the last girl skips four. She has four choices: five, six, seven, or eight.

Move the second girl to four, then five, then six. That's three choices, then two, then one.

Four, plus three, plus two, plus one. Ten good sets start in spot one.

Start in spot two: three, plus two, plus one. That's six sets.

Spot three gives two plus one, three sets. Spot four gives just one: four, six, eight.

Ten, plus six, plus three, plus one. That's twenty good sets!

Twenty over fifty-six. Divide both by four. That's five over fourteen. Choice C!

## Solution 2: boys first

Here's a faster way. Line up the five boys first.

Look at the gaps: four between the boys, plus one at each end. That's six gaps.

Two girls in the same gap would touch. So each gap gets at most one girl.

We choose three of the six gaps. Six, times five, times four, over six, like before.

That's twenty again! Twenty over fifty-six is five over fourteen. And we never needed a list.

When would you pick this one? When the line is long, and a list would take forever.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice B uses only five gaps. Three of five gaps is ten ways. Ten over fifty-six.

That's five over twenty-eight. We forgot the gap at one end!

Choice A is what you'd get with no end gaps. Three of four gaps is four ways.

Four over fifty-six is one over fourteen. But a girl CAN stand at either end!

Choice D is what you'd get from three girls over eight kids. It ignores touching.

Choice E is what you'd get if touching and not touching were equally likely.

They're not! Twenty sets are good, and thirty-six are bad.

So here's a great habit. When you count gaps, always check both ends.

## Four boys: the answer

Let's check. Four boys make three gaps between them, plus two ends. Five gaps.

Choose three of the five gaps. Five, times four, times three, over six. That's ten.

Now all the sets of spots. Seven, times six, times five, over six. That's thirty-five.

Ten over thirty-five. Divide both by five. That's two over seven! Amazing work if you got it!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Only the spots of the girls matter, and every set of spots is equally likely.

To keep the girls apart, place the boys first. Then choose gaps, one girl each, counting both ends.
