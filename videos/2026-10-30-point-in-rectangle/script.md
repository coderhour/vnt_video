# One Point, Four Triangles | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

One point inside a rectangle makes four triangles. You know three of their areas.

Can you find the fourth, without knowing where the point is? You can!

## The question

Here's the question. Point P is inside a rectangle, and it's joined to all four corners.

The triangles on the bottom, right and top sides have areas fifteen, twenty-two and twenty-five.

What is the area of the triangle on the left side?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

Let's break it down. P is joined to all four corners, so we get four triangles.

Each triangle sits on one side of the rectangle.

The bottom one has area fifteen, the right one has twenty-two, and the top one has twenty-five.

We want the area of the left one.

We don't know how big the rectangle is, or exactly where P is. That's the puzzle!

## Solution 1: base times height

Welcome back! Let's use the area rule: one over two, times base, times height.

Look at the bottom triangle and the top triangle together.

The bottom side and the top side have the same length: the width of the rectangle.

The bottom triangle's height goes from P down to the bottom side.

The top triangle's height goes from P up to the top side.

Stack those two heights, and they make the full height of the rectangle.

Add the two areas, and you get the width times the full height, divided by two.

Width times height is the whole rectangle. So the pair makes HALF the rectangle!

Watch P slide up and down. One triangle grows, and the other one shrinks.

But their total never changes. It stays half the rectangle.

The same idea works for the left and right triangles.

Their bases are the left and right sides, and their heights add up to the width.

So that pair makes the other half.

Bottom plus top is fifteen plus twenty-five, which is forty. That's half the rectangle.

So the right one plus the left one is also forty.

Twenty-two plus the left one is forty. So the left one is eighteen. The answer is C!

Let's double-check. Bottom plus top is forty, so the whole rectangle is two times forty: eighty.

Take away fifteen, twenty-two and twenty-five. That leaves eighteen. It matches!

## Solution 2: cut it into four

Here's a second way to SEE it, just by cutting.

Draw a line across through P, and a line up and down through P.

Now the big rectangle is made of four small rectangles.

The line from P to a corner cuts each small rectangle into two matching halves.

In every small rectangle, one piece belongs to the top or bottom triangle.

The other piece belongs to the left or right triangle.

So top plus bottom gets exactly half of the whole rectangle, and left plus right gets the rest.

When would you pick this one? When you want to see why the rule is true.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the traps.

Twenty-two feels right, because it sits across from the missing triangle.

But left and right only match when P is halfway across. Here, P is a bit to the left.

Twelve comes from pairing neighbours: fifteen plus twenty-two, take away twenty-five.

But neighbours don't make a pair. Fifteen plus twenty-two is thirty-seven, not forty.

Fifteen just copies the bottom triangle. Nothing makes the left one match it.

Twenty-eight is too big. The left and right pair would be fifty, but each pair must be forty.

Quick check for any answer: opposite pairs must have the same total.

## Variant: the answer

Welcome back! This time, the pair we know is left and right.

Sixteen plus twenty is thirty-six. That's half the rectangle.

So bottom plus top is also thirty-six.

Nine plus the top one is thirty-six, so the top triangle is twenty-seven.

And the whole rectangle is two times thirty-six, which is seventy-two.

Amazing work if you got both!

## Recap

Let's recap. Join a point inside a rectangle to all four corners.

Opposite triangles always add up to half the rectangle, wherever the point is.
