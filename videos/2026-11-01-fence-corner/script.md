# The Best Fence in a Corner | AMC 10-Style Level 4

## Intro

Here's a fence with one bend, tucked into a corner. How much ground can it hold?

Most kids bend it into a square. But a square is NOT the best you can do!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's find the best shape with a mirror trick.

## The question

Here's the question. Two straight walls meet at a right angle.

A fence made of two straight pieces runs from one wall to the other, bending once.

The two pieces have a total length of twelve. The fence and the walls enclose a region.

What is the largest possible area of the region? Here are the five choices.

## What are we asked?

Let's name things. Call the corner O, the fence ends A and B, and the bend P.

The walls are free. We choose where the ends go, how long each piece is, and the bend.

Bend it into a six by six square, and the area is thirty-six.

A straight fence across, with equal legs of six root two, also gives thirty-six. Can a bend do better?

## Solution 1: the mirror trick

Welcome back! Here's a neat trick: treat each wall like a mirror.

Reflect our region in the bottom wall. Now the fence has four pieces.

Now reflect everything in the side wall too. The walls vanish inside one closed shape!

It has eight fence pieces, so it's an octagon. Its perimeter is four times twelve, forty-eight.

Its area is four times our region. So we want the biggest octagon with perimeter forty-eight.

Here's a fact worth knowing. For a fixed perimeter, a circle holds the most area.

With eight straight sides, the winner is the regular octagon, with equal sides and equal angles.

Can our fence make one? Place the center of the octagon at the corner O, and turn it to fit.

Its corners are forty-five degrees apart. So the walls, ninety degrees apart, hold two sides.

Each side is forty-eight over eight, which is six. Two pieces of six: that's our fence!

Now the area. Turn the octagon so a side sits flat. It's a square with its corners cut off.

Each cut corner has equal legs and a long side of six.

In a forty-five forty-five ninety triangle, the long side is a leg times root two. So each leg is three root two.

Along the top: a leg, then six, then another leg. That adds up to six plus six root two.

Squared, that's one hundred eight, plus seventy-two root two.

Each corner triangle has area nine. So the four corners cut away thirty-six.

That leaves seventy-two plus seventy-two root two for the octagon.

Our region is one quarter of it: eighteen plus eighteen root two! That's choice B.

That's about forty-three and a half. Much better than thirty-six!

## Solution 2: tallest in the middle

Here's a second way, with just one idea: tallest in the middle.

Join A to B. With the bend pointing out, that cuts our region into two triangles.

The corner triangle has a right angle at O. So O sits on a half circle over A B.

The outer triangle has A P plus P B equal to twelve, like a loop of string pinned at A and B.

Whatever A B is, both triangles are tallest right in the middle.

So in the best shape, O A equals O B, and both fence pieces are six.

Now cut along O P. We get two matching triangles, each with an angle of forty-five degrees at O.

Stand one on its side A P. Points that see A P at forty-five degrees lie on one arc.

Tallest in the middle again! So O A equals O P.

Now A, P and B are all the same distance from O. Each piece spans forty-five degrees.

Eight of those go all the way around. It's the regular octagon again! Same answer.

When would you pick this way? When you want to see WHY the balanced shape wins.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

A is the six by six square. It fits, but it's not the biggest.

E is the whole octagon. Remember, our region is just one quarter of it!

C is thirty-six root two, about fifty-one. D is thirty-six plus eighteen root two, about sixty-one.

But no region beats forty-three and a half!

Great habit: estimate each choice, and cross out the impossible ones.

## Straight fence: the answer

Let's check. Reflect in both walls. Four sides of ten. Find the biggest one with perimeter forty.

The regular one wins again: a square with side ten, and area one hundred.

Our region is one quarter of it: twenty-five!

Check: both legs are five root two. Half of five root two, times five root two, is twenty-five.

If you found twenty-five, fantastic work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

When a fence meets straight walls, mirror it. The walls disappear, and you get a closed shape.

Same perimeter, same number of sides: the regular shape holds the most area.
