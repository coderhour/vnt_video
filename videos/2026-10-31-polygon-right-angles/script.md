## Solution 1: walk around the outside

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: walk around the outside of the shape, and add up your turns.

Walk around a square. You turn ninety degrees at each corner, four times. That's three hundred sixty.

Here's the amazing part.

You end up facing the way you started, so you've spun around once.

For any convex shape, your turns always add up to one full circle, three hundred sixty degrees.

At each corner, the inside angle and the turn sit on a straight line. Together they make one hundred eighty.

At a ninety degree corner, you turn one hundred eighty minus ninety, which is ninety degrees.

At a corner of one hundred sixty degrees, you turn just twenty degrees.

The two square corners use up ninety plus ninety, one hundred eighty degrees of turning.

That leaves one hundred eighty.

Each of the other corners turns twenty degrees. One hundred eighty divided by twenty is nine corners.

Nine corners plus the two square ones makes eleven corners, so eleven sides.

The polygon has eleven sides. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: add the inside angles

Here's another way.

A shape with n sides splits into n minus two triangles.

So its inside angles add to n minus two, times one hundred eighty.

Our shape has two ninety degree corners, and n minus two corners of one hundred sixty.

So n minus two times one hundred eighty, equals one hundred eighty, plus n minus two times one hundred sixty.

n minus two groups of one hundred eighty, take away n minus two groups of one hundred sixty, leaves n minus two groups of twenty.

And that equals one hundred eighty.

So n minus two is nine, and n is eleven. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When you like equations. The walk is quicker when lots of corners match.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A, nine, counts only the one hundred sixty degree corners. Don't forget the two square ones!

Choice E, twenty, is the turn at each of those corners, twenty degrees.

That's an angle, not a number of sides.

Choices B and D, ten and twelve, fail the angle check.

Ten sides should add to one thousand four hundred forty. But two corners of ninety and eight of one hundred sixty add to one thousand four hundred sixty.

Twelve sides should add to one thousand eight hundred. But two corners of ninety and ten of one hundred sixty add to one thousand seven hundred eighty.

So here's a great habit. For polygon angles, think about the turns. They always add up to three hundred sixty.
