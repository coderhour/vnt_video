# Pizza, Pasta, Soup: Who Likes All Three? | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Twenty-four kids, three foods. What's the fewest kids who could like all three?

You might reach for a big Venn diagram. There's a much quicker trick.

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's count the kids who say no.

## The question

Here's the question.

In a class of twenty-four kids,

Twenty kids like pizza,

Eighteen kids like pasta,

and sixteen like soup. What is the smallest possible number of kids who like all three?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Twenty-four kids, and three counts: twenty, eighteen and sixteen.

We want the smallest POSSIBLE number who like all three.

So we get to arrange who likes what, and try to make that group as small as we can.

## Solution 1: count the no's

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: count the kids who say no.

Twenty like pizza, so four kids don't.

Eighteen like pasta, so six don't. Sixteen like soup, so eight don't.

Any kid who says no to even one food is NOT in the all-three group.

To make the all-three group small, give the no's to different kids. No kid says no twice.

Then four plus six plus eight is eighteen kids who say no to something.

That leaves twenty-four minus eighteen, six kids who say no to nothing. They like all three!

Check the counts: twenty like pizza, eighteen like pasta, sixteen like soup. It works.

And we can't do better, because there are only eighteen no's to hand out.

The answer is six. That's Choice C!

## Solution 2: two at a time

Here's another way: combine two foods at a time.

Pizza and pasta: twenty plus eighteen is thirty-eight.

That's fourteen more than twenty-four, so at least fourteen kids like both.

Now add soup. Fourteen plus sixteen is thirty.

That's six more than twenty-four, so at least six like all three. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When you want to see the overlap shrink one food at a time.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice B, four, is the number of kids who don't like pizza. That's only one of the counts.

Choice E, sixteen, is the soup group.

That's the biggest the all-three group could be, not the smallest.

Choice D, fourteen, is the pizza and pasta overlap. That's what you get if you stop before adding soup.

Choice A, zero, would need twenty-four no's. There are only eighteen, so six kids must like everything.

So here's a great habit. To find the smallest group that says yes to everything, count the no's.

## Four favorites: the answer

Let's count the no's. Five don't like cats, eight don't like dogs.

Ten don't like fish, and three don't like birds.

Give them to different kids: five plus eight plus ten plus three is twenty-six no's in all.

Thirty minus twenty-six is four. At least four kids like all four!

Here's the cool part. A fourth favorite didn't make it harder, just one more count.

If you got four, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

Count how many kids say no to each thing, and give the no's to different kids.

Everyone left over must like everything. That's the smallest the group can be.
