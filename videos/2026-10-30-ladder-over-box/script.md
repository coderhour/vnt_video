# The Ladder Over the Box | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

A ladder leans over a big box and reaches a wall.

## The question

Here's the question.

A box twelve feet tall and twelve feet deep sits on the ground, against a wall.

A ladder has its foot on the ground, nine feet from the box.

It touches the top outer edge of the box, and reaches the wall.

How long is the ladder, in feet?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we really know?

The foot is nine feet from the box. The box is twelve deep, so the wall is twenty-one feet away.

The ladder just touches the box's top corner, twelve feet up.

We need the WHOLE ladder, from the ground all the way to the wall.

## Solution 1: rise over run

Welcome back! Let's look at the small triangle, from the foot to the box's corner.

Going nine feet across, the ladder rises twelve feet.

A straight ladder keeps the same steepness the whole way up.

Twelve up for nine across is the same as four up for every three across.

The wall is twenty-one feet away. That's seven groups of three.

So the ladder rises seven groups of four. Seven times four is twenty-eight.

The ladder meets the wall twenty-eight feet up!

Now we have a big right triangle. Twenty-one across, twenty-eight up.

The ladder is the long side. Let's use Pythagoras.

Twenty-one squared is four hundred forty-one. Twenty-eight squared is seven hundred eighty-four.

Add them up. That's one thousand two hundred twenty-five.

And thirty-five times thirty-five is one thousand two hundred twenty-five.

So the ladder is thirty-five feet long. The answer is D!

## Solution 2: two pieces

Here is another way. The box corner cuts the ladder into two pieces.

The lower piece: nine across, twelve up. That's the three, four, five triangle, times three.

So the lower piece is fifteen feet.

The upper piece runs over the box, twelve feet across.

Same steepness, so it rises four for every three. Twelve across means sixteen up.

Sides twelve, sixteen and twenty. That's the three, four, five triangle, times four.

So the upper piece is twenty feet. Fifteen plus twenty is thirty-five! It matches.

When would you pick this way? When the pieces are nice triangles, the numbers stay small.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Fifteen is just the lower piece. It stops at the box's corner.

Twenty is what you'd get if you only measured the upper piece, from the box to the wall.

Twenty-eight is how high the ladder reaches on the wall. That's a height, not the ladder's length.

Thirty-seven is what you'd get if you added nine across and twenty-eight up.

But you can't just add across and up. The ladder cuts the corner, so use Pythagoras.

So here's a great habit. Read the question again: the ladder goes all the way to the wall.

## New ladder: the answer

Let's check. Six across, eight up. That's four up for every three across again.

The wall is six plus nine, which is fifteen feet away. That's five groups of three.

So the ladder rises five groups of four. That's twenty feet up the wall.

Fifteen across, twenty up. Fifteen squared plus twenty squared is six hundred twenty-five.

Twenty-five times twenty-five is six hundred twenty-five. So the ladder is twenty-five feet!

Check with pieces: ten below the corner, fifteen above. Ten plus fifteen is twenty-five!

If you found twenty-five, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

A straight line rises the same amount for every step across.

So use the small triangle to find how high it goes, then use Pythagoras for the length.
