# Up the Hill, Down the Hill: Average Speed | AMC 8-Style Level 5

## Intro

Jo bikes up a hill slowly, then zooms back down. What's her average speed?

Averaging the two speeds feels right. It's a trap, and there's a second trap hiding in the units!

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's ride this one carefully.

## The question

Here's the question.

Jo bikes up a three kilometer hill at twelve kilometers per hour.

She then bikes back down the same hill at ten meters per second.

What is her average speed for the whole round trip, in kilometers per hour?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? Three kilometers up, then the same three kilometers down.

Watch out: the two speeds use different units. Kilometers per hour, and meters per second.

Average speed means total distance divided by total time.

## Solution 1: total distance over total time

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: find the total distance and the total time.

First, let's fix the units. Ten meters per second means ten meters every second.

An hour has three thousand six hundred seconds.

So in an hour she'd go ten times three thousand six hundred, thirty-six thousand meters.

That's thirty-six kilometers. So going down, her speed is thirty-six kilometers per hour.

Going up, three kilometers at twelve kilometers per hour takes a quarter of an hour. That's fifteen minutes.

Going down, three kilometers at thirty-six kilometers per hour takes a twelfth of an hour.

That's five minutes.

In total, she rides six kilometers in twenty minutes.

Twenty minutes is a third of an hour. So in a whole hour, at that pace, she'd go three times as far.

Six times three is eighteen. Her average speed is eighteen kilometers per hour.

The answer is eighteen. That's Choice B!

## Solution 2: pick a friendly hill

Here's a clever shortcut. The length of the hill doesn't change the average, because both times grow together.

So pick a friendly length, like thirty-six kilometers!

Going up at twelve takes three hours. Going down at thirty-six takes one hour.

That's seventy-two kilometers in four hours. Seventy-two divided by four is eighteen. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When the distance makes awkward fractions.

Choose one both speeds divide.

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice A, eleven, is the average of twelve and ten. That mixes kilometers per hour with meters per second.

Choice C, twenty-two, is twelve plus ten. Adding speeds doesn't make sense at all.

Choice E, thirty-six, is just the downhill speed, not the whole trip.

Choice D, twenty-four, is the average of twelve and thirty-six. The units are right, but it's still a trap!

She spends fifteen minutes going slowly and only five minutes going fast.

The slow part counts for more time, so the average is lower.

So here's a great habit. Average speed is total distance over total time. Never just average the speeds.

## Gentler hill: the answer

Let's check.

Five meters per second is five times three thousand six hundred, eighteen thousand meters an hour.

That's eighteen kilometers per hour.

Going up takes a third of an hour, twenty minutes. Going down takes a sixth of an hour, ten minutes.

In total, he rides six kilometers in thirty minutes.

Thirty minutes is half an hour, so in a whole hour he'd go twice as far. Six times two is twelve kilometers per hour!

Here's the sneaky part. Averaging nine and eighteen gives more than thirteen.

The real answer is lower, because the slow part takes longer.

If you got twelve, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

First, make the units match. Then find the total distance and the total time.

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. Don't average the two speeds!
