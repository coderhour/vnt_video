# Four Triangles in a Trapezoid | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

Two lines cut this shape into four triangles.

We know the area of just ONE of them. Can we find all four?

Hi, I'm Vantee! Let's crack it together.

## The question

Here's the question.

In trapezoid A B C D, side A B is parallel to side D C.

A B is six, and D C is ten.

The diagonals cross at point E. The shaded triangle A B E has area nine.

What is the area of the whole trapezoid?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we really know?

The diagonals split the trapezoid into four triangles: top, bottom, left and right.

We know the top one is nine. We need all four added up.

## Solution 1: four triangles

Welcome back! Let's look at the top triangle and the bottom triangle.

The top and bottom are parallel, so these two angles match. They make a Z shape.

These two match as well. That's another Z, along the other diagonal.

And the angles at E are opposite each other, so they're equal too.

Same angles make the same shape! The bottom triangle is a blown-up copy of the top one.

How much bigger? The top side is six, the bottom side is ten.

Six to ten is the same as three to five. So every length grows by five over three.

Now here's the key step. What happens to the area?

The base grows by five over three. And the height grows by five over three too.

So the area grows by five over three, TWICE. That's twenty-five over nine.

Nine times twenty-five over nine is twenty-five. The bottom triangle is twenty-five!

Now the triangle on the right, B C E. Look at it next to the top triangle.

They share the corner B, and their bases sit on the same diagonal.

So they have the same height! Their areas compare just like their bases.

In our copy, A E matches C E. So A E to E C is three to five.

So the triangle on the right is nine times five over three. That's fifteen.

The triangle on the left works the same way, using the other diagonal. It's fifteen too.

Let's add them all up. Nine, plus twenty-five, plus fifteen, plus fifteen.

That's sixty-four! The answer is D.

## Solution 2: find the height

Here's a second way. The top triangle has base six and area nine.

Half of six, times its height, is nine. So its height is three.

The bottom triangle is the five over three copy. So its height is three times five over three. That's five.

Stack them up. The whole trapezoid is three plus five, which is eight, tall.

Trapezoid area: add the top and bottom, take half, then times the height.

Six plus ten is sixteen. Half is eight. Eight times eight is sixty-four!

When would you pick this way? It's fast, and it's a great double-check.

## Common traps

Now, let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Thirty-four is just the top and bottom triangles. It forgets the two side ones.

Forty-nine counts only one side triangle, not both.

Fifty-four is what you'd get if you grew the area by five over three only once.

Sixty-eight is what you'd get if each side triangle were halfway between nine and twenty-five.

Halfway is seventeen. But the true side area is nine times five over three, which is fifteen.

So here's a great habit. Always count every piece, and square the scale for areas.

## New trapezoid: the answer

Let's check. Four to eight is one to two. So the bottom is a copy, twice as big.

Area grows by two, twice. That's four. So the bottom triangle is four times four, which is sixteen.

Each side triangle is four times two. That's eight.

Four, plus sixteen, plus eight, plus eight. That's thirty-six!

Check with heights. Half of four, times the height, is four. So the top height is two.

The bottom height is twice that, four. Two plus four is six.

Four plus eight is twelve. Half is six. Six times six is thirty-six. It matches!

If you found thirty-six, amazing work!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

The diagonals cut a trapezoid into four triangles. The top and bottom ones are copies.

Lengths grow by the scale. Areas grow by the scale, twice.
