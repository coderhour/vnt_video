# The Hidden Isosceles Triangle | AMC 8-Style Level 4

## Intro

This question gives you three side lengths. You only need one of them.

Hiding in the picture is a special triangle. Once you spot it, the answer pops right out.

## The question

Here's the question.

Triangle A B C has A B and A C both equal to ten, and B C equal to twelve.

A line through point A runs parallel to B C.

The line that cuts angle A B C exactly in half meets this line at point F.

How long is A F?

Here are the five choices. Take a good look.

## What are we asked?

So what do we know? The top line and B C never meet. They're parallel.

The line from B to F splits the corner at B into two equal angles. That's what bisector means.

We want the length of A F, the piece of the top line from A to F.

## Solution 1: chase the angles

Welcome back! Here's the strategy: chase the angles.

The bisector splits angle B into two equal parts. Let's call each part x.

Now look for a Z shape. The line B F crosses the two parallel lines.

Inside a Z, the two corner angles are equal. So the angle at F is also x!

Now look at triangle A B F. It has two equal angles, both x.

A triangle with two equal angles has two equal sides, the sides opposite those angles.

Side A F sits across from the angle at B. Side A B sits across from the angle at F. So A F equals A B.

A B is ten, so A F is ten. That's Choice D!

## Solution 2: spot the rhombus

Here's another way to see it. Let's build a shape.

Mark a point G on B C so that B G is ten, the same as A B.

Now mark the spot ten along the top line from A. Let's check that the bisector lands exactly there.

Join it up with A, B and G. The top and bottom are parallel and both ten.

Parallel and equal top and bottom make a parallelogram, so the slanted sides match too.

That's ten and ten. All four sides are ten. A rhombus!

A rhombus's diagonal cuts its corner exactly in half.

So the bisector from B goes right through that spot. It's F, ten from A. Same answer!

When would you pick this way? When you'd rather build a shape than chase angles. Both work!

## Common traps

Now let's look at the wrong answers. Each one is a trap.

Choice B, six, is half of B C. That's a real length in the picture, but not A F.

Choice E, twelve, is B C itself. A F is parallel to B C, but it isn't the same length.

Choice C, eight, is the height of the triangle, from A straight down to B C. That's a real length, but the wrong one.

Choice A, five, is what you'd get if you cut A B in half.

So here's a great habit. When you see a bisector and a parallel line, look for an isosceles triangle.

## Lopsided triangle: the answer

Let's check. The bisector splits angle B into two equal angles, x and x.

The Z shape across the parallel lines makes the angle at F equal x too.

So triangle A B F has two equal angles, and A F equals A B.

So the length of A F is seven!

Here's the sneaky part. The nine and the ten didn't matter at all. If you got seven, well done!

## Recap

Here's the big idea to remember.

A parallel line makes Z angles equal.

So when a bisector crosses a parallel line, look for a triangle with two equal angles. Two of its sides match!
